## What each pass costs the GPU, and a low tier that costs more than high (2026-09-29, perf + tools + docs, no code change)

[Volume 107](107-2026-09-28-frame-pacing.md) cut almost only CPU cost. A phone limited by its GPU
gains nothing from that. This pass measured the GPU side of the frame: what each filter pass, each
floor layer and each quality tier costs. The ranking holds across three screen sizes. It also turned
up a finding nobody asked about. **The `low` tier makes the GPU frame nearly twice as expensive as
`high` on a DPR-1 display.** It is about level with `high` everywhere else, and it is never the
cheapest tier. No game code changed, because the fix depends on a question only a real phone can
answer.

### The tool: `gpuCost.mjs`

`client/tools/perf/gpuCost.mjs` (`npm run perf:gpu-cost`) runs in the separate CDP Chrome from the
perf README.

1. It starts a run, lets enemies come on screen, and stops the ticker, so every sample renders the
   same frame.
2. It switches off one thing at a time and times the frame with `EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2`.
3. The arms are interleaved with the unchanged frame (base, arm, base, arm, …). Each arm reads as a
   paired difference from the base samples either side of it. The first round is thrown away.

Four controls decide whether a run is quoted:

- an arm that changes nothing must read under 0.1 ms;
- a calibration arm adds ten full-screen half-alpha layers and must cost something;
- an empty render must cost under a quarter of the frame;
- no sample may be disjoint.

Two of the four final runs failed the first check, and only their large rows are used below.

`--viewport WxH@DPR` emulates a screen and reloads under it, because the renderer picks its
resolution at boot. Two traps came up while building it:

- **WebGL makes a query result available only after control returns to the event loop.** The first
  version polled synchronously and hung the tab, which then had to be closed from outside. The
  existing `perf/gpuTimer.ts` has an async fallback for the same reason.
- **Clearing the emulation leaves the renderer at the emulated resolution.** A "desktop" run straight
  after a phone run measured 1264x705 at resolution 2 and read 3.2 ms instead of 1.8, with no sign in
  the output. The script now reloads the page when it finishes.

### What was measured

Intel Arc under ANGLE/D3D11, production build, a level-1 room with 8 enemies. The table gives GPU
milliseconds saved by switching each thing off. A negative number means the frame got **more**
expensive.

| Arm | Desktop 1264x705 @1 | Phone 844x390 @3 | Desktop 1264x705 @2 | fb binds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Base frame (high) | 1.81 | 1.33 | 3.49 | 15 |
| Lighting as one pass instead of the MSAA split | 0.51 | 0.13 | 0.63 | 10 |
| No lighting at all | 0.67 | 0.25 | 0.70 | 8 |
| Floor hidden | 0.42 | 0.22 | 0.34 | 15 |
| No bloom | 0.18 | 0.15 | noise | 10 |
| No vignette + chromatic | 0.11 | −0.08 | −0.27 | 13 |
| Shadows / walls and actors / far-side plane hidden | ≤ 0.15 each | ≤ 0.04 each | noise | 15 |
| **Medium tier** | 0.83 | 0.27 | 0.78 | 3 |
| **Low tier** | **−1.58** | 0.12 | 0.06 | 1 |
| Light and bloom off, world pass kept | 0.83 | 0.34 | 0.97 | 3 |
| Every pass off, resolution unchanged | **−1.58** | **−3.89** | **−8.89** | 1 |

A second desktop @1 run agrees on every large row: medium 0.92, low −1.50, every pass off −1.55. Its
no-change arm read 0.12 ms, just over the limit, so its small rows are not used. The phone column is an emulated screen on this desktop GPU, not a phone.

What it says, most important first:

- **Every filter pass keeps the world at 1x and unmultisampled.** A Pixi filter draws into a pool
  texture at resolution 1 with no MSAA (volume 107 found the second half). Switch every pass off and
  the whole scene lands in the canvas instead, which is multisampled (`antialias: true`) and at the
  full renderer resolution. At resolution 1 the MSAA alone adds 1.6 ms, nearly doubling the frame. At
  resolution 2 it adds 3.9 ms on the phone screen and 8.9 ms on the larger one. The calibration shows
  the same thing on plain quads: a full-screen layer costs 0.050 ms on the canvas against 0.031 ms
  inside the world pass at @1, and 0.058 against 0.010 at @3.
- **So the low tier is never the cheapest tier here.** Low has no passes, so it pays that whole
  penalty. Its `resolutionCap: 1` wins back the resolution half, which leaves it about level with
  high at DPR 2 and 3. At DPR 1 there is nothing to win back, and low costs 3.4 ms against high's 1.8.
  Removing the vignette pass is negative on the high-DPR screens for the same reason: without it,
  what is left of the world composites at 2x into the canvas.
- **The cheapest configuration on all three screens is one plain pass with no lighting.** That is
  "light and bloom off, world pass kept", with 3 framebuffer binds. Medium is close behind it.
- **Within high, the MSAA lighting split is the largest single cost.** It is 0.5–0.6 ms at desktop
  size and 0.13 ms on the phone screen. The floor is the largest content cost (0.2–0.4 ms). Bloom,
  the vignette pass, the shadows, the standing pieces and the far-side plane are each small.

### Why nothing was changed

A tile-based phone GPU charges differently from this one in exactly the two places that matter. It
resolves MSAA on chip, so the multisampled canvas may cost it little. It also pays for each
framebuffer bind with a tile flush, which is why `low` was built to have no passes. So on a phone,
low may well be the cheapest tier, which is what it was designed to be. That can only be settled on
a device.

What is already wrong is narrower and real. On a DPR-1 desktop or laptop whose GPU cannot hold the
high tier, `'auto'` steps from high to medium, which helps (1.8 → 1.0 ms here). If medium still does
not fit, it steps to low, which costs 3.4 ms, and the ladder never steps back up (`render/quality.ts`
`resolveTier`). A player who picks low by hand on such a machine to save battery also gets the most
expensive frame.

The options, for the owner:

1. Leave it. The devices that reach `low` are meant to be phones, and a phone may reverse the ranking.
2. Give low one plain pass on `world`. That is the cheapest arm on every screen here, at the cost of
   2 extra framebuffer binds on a tiler.
3. Choose low's form by display. Keep it pass-free where the renderer resolution is above 1, and give
   it the plain pass where it is 1, since that is where it loses. This needs no phone to justify.

### Numbers

- **GPU frame, high tier:** 1.8 ms at 1264x705 @1, 1.3 ms at 844x390 @3, 3.5 ms at 1264x705 @2.
- **Medium saves** 0.8 / 0.3 / 0.8 ms. **Low saves** −1.6 / 0.1 / 0.1 ms.
- **The canvas penalty** (every pass off at unchanged resolution): +1.6 / +3.9 / +8.9 ms.
- **Code:** `client/tools/perf/gpuCost.mjs`, the `perf:gpu-cost` script and the README section. No
  game code and no test changed.
