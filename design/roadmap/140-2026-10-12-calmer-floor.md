## A calmer floor: darker stone, softer joints, and no relief on the ground (2026-10-12, client + art + tools + test + docs, no ENGINE_VERSION change)

> 现在房间地图有个问题，我感觉地板太亮了而且缝隙特别多，移动的时候缝隙抖动导致头晕。

The report was that the floor is too bright, has too many joints, and the joints jitter while the
player moves, which makes the user dizzy. This user is prone to motion sickness, so jitter is a bug,
not polish. The warm-stone floors shipped two days earlier (volume 139) were a light beige paving
with thick dark joints. Measured on ember floor 1, the problem was not one thing: four separate
layers each drew lines on the floor, and one of them moved.

### What drew the lines

1. **The swatch itself.** Median luma 172, with joints whose 5th-percentile luma sat at 0.15-0.28
   of the median across the four chapters. At density 2.56 a tile was 200 world px, so a 1024 px
   viewport at zoom 2.13 carried a few dozen of the brightest-against-darkest edges in the frame.
2. **The lighting pass embossed them.** `litFx` builds a fake normal from the luminance gradient at
   gain 7, which is what makes a small sprite read as a body. It also ran over the floor, so every
   joint became a rim, bright on one side and dark on the other. The player carries a point light,
   so as the player walked, every rim near them was re-shaded frame by frame. That is the part that
   moved. With the relief off, the floor's total variation along a 200 px row fell from 772 to 282.
3. **The room light's bands.** 12 side-by-side strokes, each at its own alpha. Where two meet inside
   a pixel, both are antialiased to partial coverage, and two partial coverages composite to less
   than either band alone, which leaves a bright hairline at each boundary, parallel to the wall.
4. **The wall shadows.** The base hug was five 3 px strokes on 1.5/4/7/10.5/14 px centres, leaving
   0.5 px of bare floor between the third and fourth and the fourth and fifth: two light lines
   around every wall, which at zoom 2.13 is a 1 px line each. The cast shadow was four hard-edged
   hulls whose ~0.13 steps were ~23-luma lines on the light floor.

Found by A/B, not by looking. Hiding `layers.shadow` made the concentric lines beside a wall stub
vanish, and toggling the floor pass's `uGradient` showed the rims as dips lighter than both
neighbours, which nesting and blurring cannot produce and a gradient-based normal can.

### What changed

- **Art: darker, softer, larger.** `colorGrade.mjs` gained `--contrast` (pull each pixel's luma
  toward the median by a factor, RGB scaled together so hue survives) and `--soften` (mix in half of
  a wrapped box blur). All four floor masters were regraded in place with
  `--soften=2 --contrast=0.4 --median=105` and the JPEGs re-encoded at q90 (24-30 KB, down from
  55-80). Floor density 2.56 → 1.6, so a tile covers 320 world px and ~40% fewer joints are on
  screen. The user picked this from four live frames: current, a medium grade, the dark grade, and
  the dark grade with larger slabs.
- **The floor is lit flat.** `FxController.sceneLightFloor` is a third `SceneLightFilter` with
  gradient 0, mounted on `layers.litFloor` when the high tier splits the pass, and fed the same
  region and lights every frame. A flat texel normalises to exactly 1.0 either way, so this removes
  rims and changes no tone. Moving every light 4 world px changed the floor's high-frequency detail
  by 0.116 per pixel with it, against 0.175 with the relief. The medium tier lights floor and actors
  in one pass and keeps the relief there: splitting would cost the pass that tier exists to save.
- **Nested shapes for every graduated darkening on the floor** (`scene/nestedRings.ts`). Each shape
  is anchored at the same source edge, so a band boundary is one shape's edge, and `nestedAlphas`
  peels the per-shape alphas from the outermost band in so the composite lands exactly on the
  intended profile. The room light now uses 24 nested rings (max step 0.034 at the warm-stone edge,
  from 0.064). The base hug uses five nested rings on contiguous edges. The cast shadow uses six
  nested hulls falling linearly from 0.43 to a sixth of it. Eight measured 62,640 of the shadow
  layer's 64,000-float budget, so the count is six.

Together, on the same room and rows: mean floor luma on screen 153 → 110, edge contrast 10.66 → 2.40
per pixel.

### Tests

- `warmStoneArt.test.ts`: the floor's tonal rule is now "mid-tone, still the lightest stone" (median
  95-120, above the face; the cap under both the face and 0.75 of the floor), plus a new rule that
  the joints are soft (5th-percentile luma over 0.6 of the median, which the old masters fail at
  0.15-0.28). The four-floors-apart check now compares luma-normalised colour, because darkening
  every floor by ~0.6 shrank the raw RGB distance (storm and blight 14 → 10) while their hue distance
  went 0.089 → 0.092. The density test pins 320 world px.
- `nestedRings.test.ts`: every band composites back to its target, to 12 places.
- `roomLight.test.ts`: the composite lands on the t² ramp, every ring is anchored at the room edge,
  and neighbouring bands differ by less than 0.04. `wallDarkening` composites the hug rings rather
  than taking their maximum.
- `FxController.test.ts`: the floor pass has gradient 0 and the other two do not, it receives the
  same region and lights, and the mounted-pass assertions are identity checks now. The old `toEqual`
  could not tell two stub filters apart, so swapping the floor pass for `sceneLight` passed all of
  them. A mutation putting `sceneLight` back on the floor now fails three.
- `groundGeometryBudget.test.ts`: the light pools 17,280 → 34,560 floats, the layer's `rest`
  232,342 → 249,622, the worst camera 26,226 → 27,090, the shadow layer's largest piece
  48,720 → 55,680. A light pool is now 576 floats, just over the 400-float auto-batch cutoff.
- `colorGrade.test.mjs`: contrast lifts the mortar and leaves the stone, keeps hue, runs before the
  median lands and is the identity at 1; soften softens an edge, leaves a flat field alone, wraps and
  never touches alpha.

### Not done

- **Where two walls' shadows overlap, the overlap is darker.** Measured beside a wall stub as
  repeated 1-2 px steps. This predates the pass and is a union problem, not a banding one.
- **The medium tier keeps relief on the floor**, for the reason above. (Its moving half went with the
  next entry's `pointRelief: 0`; the key light's static relief remains.)
- **Door light bands** (`doorLights.ts`: recess, through, rim) are still side by side. They are small
  and sit in the doorway, and were not part of what the report pointed at.

## One stone: calmer walls, and pillars painted from the walls (2026-10-12, client + art + test + docs, no ENGINE_VERSION change)

> 把房间里的墙和柱子也一起处理一下。
>
> 我觉得墙和柱子的图片本身也有点不匹配，你觉得呢
>
> 之前的方案不一定好。你综合评估一下，给我最好的最终效果

The same day, after the floor: the walls and pillars, and then whether their art matched. It did
not. Against the key frame, the room showed three stones. The wall face was a bright orange rubble
with near-black joints, the busiest surface left (8.3 total variation per pixel on screen against
the new floor's 1.0). The wall top was small regular grey brick where the key frame has large slabs.
The pillars were still the 2026-08-20 sprite authored for the charcoal walls: a smooth drum at
median 43, one file for every chapter. Both wall surfaces were also embossed by the moving lights,
since they share the stand pass with the actors.

The user asked for the best overall result rather than one of the options first offered (a code
textured cylinder, a regenerated sprite, or a retint), so the choice was made on the frame.

### What changed

- **Faces and tops regraded**, all four chapters: `--soften=2 --contrast=0.45`, plus `--desat=0.3`
  on the face; face medians kept, top median 72-75 -> 67 so its on-screen mean stayed put (79.5 ->
  80.5). Joints at 0.60-0.73 of the median. JPEGs 21-31 KB, about half.
- **Top slabs twice as large**: `wall_<el>` density 8 -> 4 (two cap cells per tile).
- **`SceneLightFilter.pointRelief`**, a new uniform scaling the relief the point lights see; 0 on
  the stand pass and on the medium tier's single pass. The key light keeps the relief, which is
  static and pans with the art. Moving every light 4 world px: face fine-detail change 0.365 ->
  0.147 per pixel, top 0.254 -> 0.177, against the relief-free floor's 0.19.
- **Pillars painted from the wall art** (`scene/pillarBake.ts`, new): the chapter's face unrolled
  around the drum by arc length with every column dropped by `capRy·cos θ`, so the courses curve
  and the foot curves with them; lit from the west at the hand-toned cylinder's terminator; the
  walls' silhouette, coping and contact crease; the wall-top swatch foreshortened onto the top.
  Painted once per chapter at 4 texels per world px into the sprite box the old sprite used, drawn
  as one sprite with mips, so occlusion, shadow and batching are as before.
  `roomDressing.buildPillarEntities` takes it for a warm-stone chapter and keeps the sprite for
  `neutral` and for a host without a 2D canvas.

On the same frame: face 8.53 -> 3.69 and top 6.94 -> 2.74 total variation per pixel.

### Tests

- `pillarBake.test.ts` (new): the painted box is the box `pillarArtExtent` measures; transparent
  outside the drum; the foot curves; a joint drops by `capRy(1 - cos θ)` toward the centre; the face
  is unrolled by arc length (a red-only stripe pattern, shading divided out, crowds toward the
  limbs); west lit, east dark; the crease; the limb edge; the top is the cap swatch with a lit back
  rim; and the canvas plumbing through a fake `DOMAdapter` canvas, including memoisation and four
  failure paths. A nine-mutation battery against the painter killed all nine; the first version of
  the unroll test let its mutation through (the dark east limb hid stripes) and was rewritten.
- `roomDressing.test.ts` (new): which body a pillar gets, the bake's inputs, no tint and no flat
  crease on the painted sprite, the occluder measured off the painted texture, and five reasons not
  to paint.
- `sceneLightModel.test.ts`: the model carries `pointRelief`; at 0 a point light shades a slope as
  it shades a flat texel while the key light still shows relief; the mirror pins the new GLSL line.
- `FxController.test.ts`: the stand and single passes run `pointRelief` 0.
- `warmStoneArt.test.ts`: the wall top covers 128 world px; the walls' joints are soft (5th
  percentile over 0.55 of the median).
- `wallComposition.test.ts`: the softened joints are near-ties, so "darkest row in the top third"
  no longer picks the first joint (ice: 66 vs 65). For warm faces the crown row is now held to being
  the darkest row of a real joint, under 0.85 of both courses; the rows themselves did not move.

### Not done

- **The wall face is still rubble, the key frame's is coursed brick.** A regrade can calm it and
  move its hue; it cannot change its pattern. That would be new art.
- **The pillar top is the wall top foreshortened**, slabs and all. A real round column would have
  one stone or radial joints on top; at ~40 px on screen this reads correctly, and it keeps the top
  the walls' own stone.
