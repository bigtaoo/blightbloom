## The gun steps onto the line its own bullets fly along (2026-09-02, client only)

*"look at the last few shots — with no monsters around you can clearly see the bullet drift in an arc after leaving the muzzle, and only then fly straight."*
Handed over with a replay file. The third report in a row about this one correction — after
[the gun never moved](11-2026-08-28--08-31.md#the-gun-never-moved-and-the-flash-was-aimed-at-the-floor-2026-08-30-client--engine)
(08-30, the fx were being burst at the sim's muzzle point) and
[a slow bullet's curve](11-2026-08-28--08-31.md#a-slow-bullets-curve-budgeted-by-distance-instead-of-a-clock-2026-08-30-client)
(08-30, the ease budget was a clock instead of a distance) — and the first one to be about
the shape of the path rather than about where it starts or how long it takes.

### What the recorded run said

The file replayed at `ENGINE_VERSION` 50 against a 51 engine, so the one sim file the bump
had touched was checked out at the older commit for the measurement (`DoorSystem.ts`; `git
show --stat` first, so this took minutes rather than a whole worktree). 63 player shots, and
the last two — ticks 1927 and 2357, the tick the recorder was fired at — were a `blaster`
aimed **straight up the screen with no enemies alive**, exactly the described scene.

Fed through the shipped `Bullet` and the shipped muzzle chain, frame by frame:

```
D = (-20.8, +0.9) world px  →  20.8 px PERPENDICULAR to the shot
f2: 43° off-aim   f3: 39°   f4: 34°   f5: 29°
f6: 23°           f7: 16°   f8: 8°    f9: 1°   → 0° thereafter
```

The round left the barrel **43° off its own aim** and slid 20.8 world px sideways over nine
render frames (~150 ms, ~43 px of travel) before flying straight. At the room camera's
~3.4–4.5x that is **~79 screen px of drift**. Not an illusion, and not a tuning matter.

### Why a correction across the shot can only be a curve

`Bullet.setMuzzleOrigin` draws a round at the shooter's real barrel tip and eases the offset
onto the sim's authoritative position. Split that offset into its two components and they
have completely different visual consequences:

- **along the shot** — the round appears to travel a little fast or a little slow. Invisible.
- **across the shot** — the only way to spend it is to move the drawn round sideways *while
  it flies forward*. That is an arc, at whatever rate it is eased. Easing it more gently
  spreads the same drift over more of the flight; it cannot remove it.

So the fix is not in the ease. Both sources of a cross-track offset were removed where they
arose, and the ease is left with a pure along-track distance:

- **Sideways.** The active weapon socket sat at its **rest angle** and only the module *spun*
  to the aim, so the bone tip it hangs off was a fixed body-local point — 52 authoring px out
  on the hero's right. Fire straight up and the drawn gun is a socket-length off the line its
  bullets fly along. `rigWeaponMount.orbitActiveSocketToAim` now turns the **bone** to the aim
  before FK runs, i.e. it actually orbits, which is what `13`'s *"two weapon modules that
  orbit it"* described all along. The enemy `held` mount lost its 0.45 vertical squash at the
  same time and for the same reason: unsquashed, in the ground plane, is not a taste call —
  `Entity.applyTransform` draws the ground 1:1 (`screen.y = groundY - z`), so anything else
  puts a drawn gun off its own bullets' line.
- **Height.** `bulletZ` is a gameplay band (0.5 grid — *"chest height, clears ground-hug
  hazards, blocked by tall cover"*, `07`) while the gun is drawn wherever the art hangs it:
  16 vs 26.4 world px for the hero. That gap points straight up the screen, so it is
  perpendicular to every horizontal shot. `muzzlePos()` now reports its own height alongside
  its point — they were always asked together — and `Bullet.setDrawnHeight` draws the round at
  the gun's height. Render-only: `z` is untouched and the sim never sees it.

What is left is the blaster's +9.5 px of extra reach, along the shot, which is what
`muzzleParity`'s table has measured since 08-30. `setMuzzleOrigin` also **projects** whatever
it is handed onto the shot direction now, so *"a bullet is never bent"* is a property of the
renderer rather than an outcome of how well two content tables happen to agree.

which is the moment the construction stops being the guarantee.

### The test gap, in two layers

**`muzzleParity.test.ts` measured a rotating chain at exactly one pose.** The file's own
header said so plainly — *"Everything is evaluated at aim = 0, the one pose where the whole
chain is a single scalar reach along the aim ray"* — and that sentence reads as a
simplification when it is the blind spot: at aim 0 the disagreement is almost entirely
along-track, and the component that draws an arc is nearly zero for reasons that have nothing
to do with the art being right. It now sweeps **24 aim angles × every ranged weapon × every
carrying body**, bounds the perpendicular component at 0.1 world px, carries a control that
fires the same measurement on the old geometry (20.8 px), and flies a real `Bullet` through
the reported shot end to end (0° off-aim where it measured 43°).

**`rigComposition.test.ts` gained the attachment invariants** — relationships between things
the renderer positions *independently*, over the real shipped bundles, swept across 16 aim
directions and every armed body:

- the module and its own socket ring are the same point (two independent placement paths);
- and stay so through every shipped clip, so the gun rides the hover bob with its ring;
- the drawn tether's own far endpoint reaches the module, read out of the signature
  `drawTethers` builds from the curve it actually strokes rather than recomputed;
- a held (mob) gun sits at the **same** distance from its body in every direction — the
  invariant the 0.45 squash broke;
- plus the anti-vacuity control: the module has to actually travel with the aim, or every
  "stays attached" check above passes for free.

## The character gets an attack, and the sim finally says when (2026-09-02, engine + client + art, ENGINE_VERSION 51→52)

A question rather than a report — *"does the character have attack animations yet — for shooting, and for holding a blade"* — and the honest
answer was **half a shooting one, and nothing at all for a blade**. A shot played a procedural
recoil envelope (2026-08-30). A swing played nothing, and could not have: **the sim never told
the render layer a swing happened.** `deflect` fires only when a swing catches a bullet and `hit`
only when it connects, so a sword swung at empty air produced no event of any kind.

Three things were missing, and they are three different kinds of missing.

**1. The enemy bundles had no clips to play.** `boss-core`, `brute-core`, `critter-core` and
`floater-core` shipped `idle`/`hurt`/`death`/`spawn` — no `move`, no `attack`. The missing `move`
was a live bug of its own, not just a gap: `Actor` has asked for `'move'` while an actor is moving
since the rig runtime landed, and `RigSkin.playClip` resolves an unknown name to **no clip at
all** — so a walking mob fell through to a bare rest pose and lost even its idle bob. All four
gained both clips, authored to the same shape the hero bundles use (a 0.6 s two-hop gait; a 0.35 s
wind-up → lunge → settle), plus the boss's own version of each in its shard rings, since its
weapon mount mode is `'none'` and the rings are its armament.

**2. The `attack` clips could not be played, and that was structural.** All three `char_*` bundles
had carried one since the rig landed and it had never been played once. Clips are sampled WHOLE,
and `playClip` swapped `this.clip` outright — so playing `attack` dropped every bone it does not
track back to rest for its whole duration. `render/rigClipLayer.ts` is the additive layer the
2026-08-30 note predicted would be needed: `rotation`/`translate` ADD, `scale`/`alpha` MULTIPLY,
and a bone the overlay never names comes through untouched. The base clip keeps running
underneath, so `socket_l` keeps its hover bob through a shot and a `move` cycle keeps walking
through an attack.

That buys one authoring rule, and it is a claim about DATA that no correct code can rescue: **an
attack clip must start and end at identity**, or the layer steps the pose on the frame it triggers
and again on the frame it expires. `rigComposition.test.ts` asserts it per bundle, along with the
full six-clip vocabulary, that every attack bone exists on its own rig, and that every looping clip
returns to its own first pose.

**3. A blade cannot be swung by a clip, for two independent reasons.** A clip's `translateX` is
applied in RIG space (`sprite.x = pose.ex + transform.translateX`), so an authored `-10` slides the
gun *left*, not backwards along its own barrel — which is exactly what the hero's original `attack`
clip did, at exactly `RECOIL_MODULE_PX`'s magnitude, so the two would have doubled up; that
translate was dropped from the clip and the assertion that it stays gone is in the composition
suite. And the weapon sockets are **aim-tracking bones**: `RigSkin` overwrites their rotation with
the aim angle every frame, so an authored swing arc is discarded in silence. `rigRecoil.ts` became
`rigAttackMotion.ts` and grew the melee half — a three-phase arc (cock 22° behind the aim, sweep to
46° ahead, recover to exactly the aim line) plus a forward lunge, against the gun's backward kick
and lean. `bodyPx` is one SIGNED number for both kinds so the renderer keeps one formula.

**The engine change, and why it is an event.** `WeaponFireSystem`'s melee branch now emits
`melee_swing`, with the identical field list to `bullet_fired` so the render layer answers both
with one reaction (`Actor.onAttack(kind)`). It could have been a render-side read of
`weapon.justSwung`, which is already hashed sim state — and that would have been wrong: it is a
**one-tick latch**, and `GameLoop.advanceOnline` drains every confirmed frame the server has ready
and then reconciles the scene ONCE, against the last of them. Every swing on an intermediate frame
would be invisible, at a rate set by latency. `session.drive()` returns the whole drained batch.

**The bump is measured, not assumed, and the measurement only works before it.** This engine
serializes `version: ENGINE_VERSION` into the hashed state, so bumping alone moves all five golden
hashes and a post-bump run cannot tell "the event changed the sim" from "the version changed". Run
against the **v51** fixture with the emit in place: all five hashes still matched, and the only
failures were the five `witness` assertions, each gaining one key — `melee_swing` at 7 / 27 / 36 /
44 / 67 across the scenarios. So nothing diverges; the bump is what `versionContract.test.ts`
demands before the witness re-record is accepted, and it is still right, because a v51 and a v52
client disagree about what a stream *emits*. Those counts are also the first evidence the golden
set exercises the melee path at all.

**Two files went back under the 500-line convention rather than into the baseline.**
`canonicalAimRad` — the pre-mirror space every body-space offset must be stated in — moved from a
`RigSkin` private to `facing.ts`, where the mirror rule already lives, and got its own tests for
the first time (four call sites depend on it and none of them could reach it before). The
module-contact repaint moved to `rigShading.ts` beside the painter it delegates to, form ①. Net:
`RigSkin.ts` 500 → 481.

**Testing: 86 new cases, and 35 mutants across two batteries.** Client 4221 / engine 1104, both
green.

The first battery — 15 mutants over the attack path — matters more for how it ran than for what it
found. Its first attempt **crashed inside the test runner after applying mutant #1 and before
reverting it**, leaving a live `scaleX` override in the source; every row printed after that was

- *the world-px readability check.* A single `or` across three channels, so scaling every enemy

- The third is a true equivalent, recorded rather than dressed up: `AttackMotion.advance`'s

**Eight of that second battery are DATA mutants — edits to the shipped `animation.json` files,
which no source-level test can reach.** All eight die: an attack clip that stops ending at

hand-maintained copy of, one bundle's attack changing duration, a clip naming a bone its rig does

lives.

**Also found and fixed while checking that the new clips were reproducible:**
`tools/animator/scripts/build-critter-project.mjs` had drifted from the bundle it writes in two
ways at once — it re-emitted `body.png` from the raw 1254 px source over the shipped 256 px
downsample (52 KB → 1 MB in the run pack), and re-derived the binding scale against that raw
source, shrinking the critter to a fifth of its drawn size. Both were caught immediately by
`rigComposition.test.ts` once the regenerated file was used, which is the only reason this is a
footnote instead of a shipped regression. The script now treats the shipped bundle as ground truth
for both — the same conclusion `build-character-projects.mjs` had already reached about its own
`orbWorldWidth`, and that script now sources its `animations` from the shipped bundle too, so a
re-run can no longer silently revert clips authored against it.

**Still open, deliberately.** `hurt`/`death`/`spawn` remain authored-and-unplayed in every bundle:
they need GameState signals `Actor` does not receive yet, which is a different piece of wiring from
this one. A swing has no audio cue of its own — `deflect` covers the parry and there is no whoosh
in the catalogue. And the idle↔move base-clip swap still has no cross-fade, so `move`'s constant
10° shell lean pops on the frame a character starts or stops walking; the new enemy `move` clips
are authored to start and end at identity specifically so they do not make that worse.

## The other three clips finally get a signal (2026-09-02, client only)

The follow-up the entry above named as still open: *"hook up the character's remaining animations:hurt / death /
spawn"*. All seven bundles have shipped all six clips — `idle`/`move`/`attack`/`hurt`/`death`/
`spawn` — since earlier the same day, and three of them had been played. These three never had,
and the reason was never the art. It was that `Actor` received no signal for any of them.

All three signals already existed, which is why this is a pure client pass with no
`ENGINE_VERSION` bump:

| clip | signal | driver |
|---|---|---|
| `hurt` | the `hit` event's `target` | `EventReactor` → `Actor.onHurt` |
| `death` | the id dropping out of the engine's alive list | `Scene.reconcile` → `Actor.onDeath` |
| `spawn` | a new id appearing, i.e. the view being built | `Scene.spawn` → `Actor.onSpawn` |

Two of the three are **diffs of `GameState`, not events**, and `Scene` is the only thing that
computes those diffs — so it drives them, and `EventReactor`'s host interface deliberately does
not grow them. `Actor` now carries the full set of four reaction methods (`onSpawn` / `onHurt` /
`onAttack` / `onDeath`), one call per signal from one place, which is the shape the attack pass
established; `hitFlash`/`startDissolve` were renamed into it, because each now drives a clip as
well as a shader.

### The layer each clip lands on was decided by the data, not by taste

The attack pass's additive contract says an overlay contributes its own first pose on the frame it
triggers and its own last pose on the frame it expires. Read in the negative, that is a test the
shipped keyframes either pass or fail — and it was checked against all three rig families before
anything was written:

- **`hurt` starts and ends at identity** on all seven bundles → it can be an additive overlay, on
  exactly the same path as `attack`. It also *should* be: a flinch must not interrupt a walk.
- **`spawn` opens at scale 0.2 / alpha 0**; **`death` ends at scale 0.4×0.3, translateY 18,
  alpha 0** → neither can be. Layered, a spawning body pops to 20% on the trigger frame and a
  corpse pops back to full size the instant its collapse finishes.

So the base layer stopped being an idle/move pick and became a small state machine,
**spawn → idle/move → death**. A lifecycle clip outranks the caller's ground clip for its
duration; `spawn` releases it back, `death` **holds its last frame**, because a corpse has nothing
to return to. `Actor` still asks for `'idle'`/`'move'` every frame and never learns which one-shot
is in flight. The single attack-overlay field became a SET rather than a second named field, which
costs nothing: both channel operations (add, multiply) are commutative, so two live overlays — a
player shot at while firing — compose to the same pose in either order and need no priority rule.
That commutativity is asserted rather than argued, since it is what would quietly break if a
channel ever became an override.

**Death is absorbing**: it refuses every later trigger and clears whatever overlays were live —
a corpse does not flinch. Written believing a killing blow's `hit` and a splash on a dissolving
actor would both reach the view; the gap audit below established that neither can, because the tick
reconciles before it consumes events. The rule stands where it is and is tested there; what changed
is the claim made for it.

### Who owns the death, given that a dissolve shader already did

Two mechanisms now run at once, and the boundary is load-bearing: the authored `death` clip owns
**the body's own collapse** (squash, sink, fade — per body plan), and `ActorFilters` owns **the
dissolve shader plus the clock that ends the view** (`isDissolved`, which `Scene` destroys on).
Art gets no vote on view lifetime. Because the clip (900 ms) is deliberately longer than
`DISSOLVE_MS` (700), the corpse is always destroyed while still visibly collapsing, so the two
read as one continuous motion rather than a collapse followed by a static body being eroded —
asserted as a relationship between the two numbers, not restated as a third.

### The drawn marks that had to learn to follow the body

Which of a rig's non-bone marks already tracked its bone was only settled by asking what `spawn`'s
alpha and `death`'s scale actually *reach* — a different question from "does the clip play". The
tether and the module contact shade already read their bone's clip alpha. Two did not:

- **the mounted weapon module** is a sprite parented to the rig, not a bone, so nothing faded it.
  `ModuleMount` carries its mount bone's clip alpha now. Without it, a spawning character mounts a
  fully-opaque gun on an arm that has not arrived, and a corpse leaves one hanging crisp over the
  dissolve. The tether out to it and its contact shade on the core already read that same bone's
  alpha — so the module was the only one of the three that had been missed.
- **the sphere shading** took the body bone's position and alpha but never its scale. Fine for a
  60 ms squash accent; not fine for a body collapsed to a third and *held* there for the whole
  dissolve, which reads as a dark plate lying beside a shrinking corpse. Position, alpha and scale
  now all come off that one bone, times the screen-space counter-flip — the one property that
  deliberately does *not* come from the bone, because the key light must not mirror with the body.
  Moved to `rigShading.placeSphereShade` (form ①) beside the painter it belongs to, which also
  kept `RigSkin.ts` at 482 of its 500 lines instead of pushing it over.

### One real authoring bug, and it was invisible in every still

The three `char_*` bundles' `death` clip named `belly`/`socket_l`/`socket_r` **only in its final
keyframe**, at alpha 0. `sampleClip` resolves a bone with no keyframe at-or-before `t` straight to
its earliest FUTURE keyframe, with no interpolation — so on frame ONE of the collapse a hero's
belly and both weapon modules vanished outright, while the shell collapsed over the following
900 ms. The clip's own last keyframe looked entirely correct, no source-level test could reach it,
and no still frame would show it.

It was found by the *identity* assertion above rather than by looking: `departure(death, 0)` came
back 1 on three bundles where the design said it had to be 0. Fixed by giving those three bones the
eye's own alpha ramp, and the general rule is now asserted for every clip in every bundle: **every
bone a clip animates must have a keyframe at `t = 0`**, or it cuts instead of ramping.

### Verified live, on a real run, through the render tree

The pane cannot present frames on this box (the previous entry has the measurement), so the render
TREE is the evidence again — driven through `window.__game` against a real offline run, with the
loop stepped by hand because a hidden pane throttles rAF. Zero console errors throughout.

- **spawn.** On the first room's first frames, **all 9 actors** (the hero and 8 mobs) read

  the sphere shade's own scale 0.52–0.73 and its alpha matching the body's to three decimals, and
  the mounted module's alpha matching it exactly. 350 ms later every one of them was back at
  alpha 1, `scale.y` 0.313 (hero) / 0.391 (mob), shade 1, module 1 — released, not held.
- **hurt.** A real `hit` through the real `EventReactor`: **nothing on the trigger frame** (the

  recovering — while the body's `y` marched −43.11 → −43.71 the whole time, i.e. the base clip

- **death.** A real alive-flag diff through `Scene.reconcile`: identity on frame 0, then `scale.y`

  the shade tracking at 0.455 / 0.387 and the module at 0.387. The view was then destroyed by the
  DISSOLVE clock while the clip still read 0.39 alpha — still visibly collapsing when it goes,
  which is exactly the relationship the two durations were chosen for.

### The gap audit afterwards, and the ordering fact it turned up

Asked *"are there tests worth adding"* once the pass was green, and re-reading the code
rather than the tests found six branches nothing reached. **+26 cases (4350), 11 targeted mutants,
11 killed** — and five of those rows failed exactly ONE test, which is the evidence that each
closes a real gap rather than duplicating cover.

What was missing, and why each one is reachable in play:

- **an overlay folded onto a LIFECYCLE base.** `sample()` builds its pose from the lifecycle clip
  when there is one and folds overlays onto whatever it built — a different branch from every
  overlay case, and it is the frame a mob is shot at while still materialising, which the arena's
  per-room encounters make routine. Every existing overlay case layered over `idle`.
- **a zero-length frame with a lifecycle in flight.** `Skin.setFacing(…, 0)` runs at
  construction and a paused frame can deliver a 0; retiring on it would skip the first frame of
  every spawn — the one where the body is smallest. Covered for overlays, not for lifecycle.
- **the degenerate `hold` inputs.** A zero-duration `spawn` must clear and a zero-duration `death`
  must hold, exactly as they diverge at full length: a latched spawn is a permanently 20%-scale
  character, a released death is a corpse standing back up.
- **releasing into nothing.** `playBase` resolves an unknown name to no clip (what a bundle missing
  `move` does), so a spawn can finish with nothing to release into. It must hand back an empty pose,
  not keep drawing its own last frame.
- **`placeSphereShade` had no direct tests at all** — only coverage through `RigSkin`, where the
  four properties can only be read together. Eight unit cases now move one channel at a time,
  including the two combinations no shipped clip produces alone: the counter-flip composed with the
  squash, and the one-way-hide bug (`visible` is set on BOTH paths, or a single unposed frame is
  permanent).
- **the LOW quality tier.** `actorShaders` is false there, so no `DissolveFilter` exists — but
  the `death` clip is not a shader, and a cheap tier is meant to lose the erosion and keep the
  animation. Nothing crossed the two, and the failure would have been invisible: the body would
  still fade on schedule, just without ever collapsing. (A tier gate is where a wired feature has
  gone untested in this repo before.)
- **two DATA claims**: `hurt`'s squash against the same dimensionless readability bar the attack
  block uses, and the fact that the three `char_*` bundles ship **byte-identical** animations —
  the hero sibling of the existing critter/brute/floater check, and they are one rig with three sets
  of art written by one script that has drifted from its own output before.

**The finding is an ordering fact nothing had ever stated.** `GameLoop` reconciles the scene
**before** it consumes the tick's events, on both the offline and online paths. So on the tick an
actor dies, `reconcile` has already moved its view out of `Scene.views` and started its collapse by
the time the `hit` that killed it reaches `EventReactor` — and `actorAt` searches nothing else.
A killing blow's hit-flash and `hurt` flinch therefore never land, and **`ClipLayers`' own "a corpse
does not flinch" rule is currently unreachable from its one live caller.**

That is not a reason to delete the guard — it is a reason to stop describing it as if it were
holding the line. It is now written and tested as a property of the class that owns the rule, with
the three facts that make it moot each asserted in their own suite: the order (both paths, by
invocation order), `actorAt` refusing a dying view, and the dying list still receiving its frame dt
so the collapse plays. Widen `actorAt` or reorder the tick and the guard becomes live, which is
exactly the shape worth a test rather than a comment. Two code comments were corrected in the same
pass for overstating what they knew — the guard's reachability, and a `Scene` comment that gave
`after snap()` as the reason `onSpawn` is called where it is, when the load-bearing fact is only
that it is after CONSTRUCTION (the constructor measures the rest pose, and the spawn clip opens at
20% scale).

### Two seams left in, on purpose

The frame `death` takes over the base layer, the ground clip's pose is dropped, so a body caught at
the top of its idle bob steps up to 6 authoring px (~2 world px); same for the frame `spawn`
releases. That is the same order as the step every existing idle↔move swap already takes (one
monotonic clock, two different clips), so it is not new and not worth a cross-fade on its own — and
the death case is covered by a shake, a debris burst and a dissolve all starting on that frame. The
cross-fade stays the open item it already was. Also still open: a swing has no audio cue of its
own, and none of `hurt`/`death`/`spawn` has one either.

## The four clips finally get a sound (2026-09-02, client + assets + tooling, no engine bump)

The follow-up the entry above named as still open, in its own words: *"a swing has no audio cue of
its own, and none of `hurt`/`death`/`spawn` has one either."* All four exist now. Nothing in the
engine moved — audio is pure presentation (design/06/11), and every signal these cues need was
already reaching the client, which is why the whole pass is client, assets and Python.

The set went **50 files / 101.9 kB → 61 files / 122.7 kB**, inside a 160 KiB budget that was
deliberately not raised.

### The five cues, and the fifth is a rename

| cue | trigger | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| `swing` | the `melee_swing` event | the melee `muzzle` — fires whether or not the stroke connects |
| `hurt` | a `hit` whose target is the LOCAL seat | "that one was on ME", layered under `impact` |
| `death.enemy` | a `death` event, `faction === 'enemy'` | **renamed** from the bare `death` |
| `death.player` | a `death` event on the local seat | the counterpart of `win` |
| `spawn` | an actor VIEW being built — no event at all | a wave materialising |

The rename is the one that was not asked for and is the one worth explaining. The bare `death`
cue was only ever played inside `if (e.faction === 'enemy')`, so the moment a run ends — the local
player bleeding out through `ReviveSystem` — was **the one lifecycle event in the game with no
sound at all**, and nothing in the union said so. It also made a second bug possible and actual:
the `downed` case played `cue('death')`, i.e. an enemy explosion crunch, for the local player
collapsing. Two named cues turn a silent branch into a decision, and they are design/11's own
written vocabulary (`death.<enemy/player>`) finally built.

`downed` now plays `hurt`, gated on the local seat. Going down IS damage; `hurt` already outranks
every other combat cue; and in the frame where both land it coalesces with the `hit` that caused
it into ONE louder voice, which is what that moment should sound like. Deliberately not
`death.player` — a downed player can be revived in co-op, so a run-ending fall there would be a
lie. The fx stays ungated, because a teammate collapsing is worth seeing.

### `spawn` is the only cue in the game with no engine event behind it

An id appearing in `GameState` is a **diff**, not an announcement, and `Scene` is the only thing
that computes diffs — which is already why `Actor.onSpawn` is driven from there rather than from
`EventReactor` (the previous entry established that rule for the clip; this pass inherits it for
the sound). So `Scene.reconcile` reports `spawnedActors`, the number of actor views it built,
`GameLoop` reads it immediately after the reconcile, and `EventReactor.consume(events, count)`
drops it into the same coalescing map every other cue passes through.

A count, not N calls, and that is the whole reason it can be a number at all: a room's wave
materialises up to nine bodies on one frame, and design/11's rule for ten hits in a frame — one
voice at higher gain, not ten — applies unchanged. Bullets and pickups are excluded because only
an `Actor` has a `spawn` clip to match; counting bullets would make `spawn` a second, louder
`muzzle` firing on the wrong frame.

### A source pool that ran out, and the second kind of source it forced

`swing` is the first cue in this project that **no Kenney pack can serve**. Listing all 323 files
across the six packs finds no whoosh, no swoosh, no air-movement family of any kind. A fixed
inventory cannot answer "does this sound exist"; only a queryable one can, which is the same
conclusion the sibling project `funny` reached from the other end when it added freesound.org
(`tools/audio-pipeline/fetch_freesound.py` there).

The new source here is **BigSoundBank** — CC0, no account, OGG served anonymously — behind a new
`tools/audio-pipeline/fetch_bigsoundbank.py`. `funny`'s lesson about that class of source
transferred verbatim in a different mechanism: **the search takes one noun.** `"sword whoosh"`
returns nothing, `"whoosh"` returns twelve; it fails silently and reads exactly like "this sound
does not exist here". `q` is therefore a list of single-word queries merged by sound id, the same
shape `funny` landed on because freesound ANDs its terms.

Three things differ from a zip pack, and none of them is an exemption:

- **No archive to hash.** So the integrity record moves per-file: every BigSoundBank asset carries
  a `source_sha256` in `credits.json`, and `platform/audioAssets.test.ts` requires it for any pack
  marked `per_sound` *and* checks it against the archived bytes. That is narrower and stronger
  than a zip hash — it covers the exact bytes that were processed.
- **The licence is on the page, not in the bundle.** `--license` captures the statement verbatim
  into `art/audio/licenses/bigsoundbank-LICENSE.txt` with its source URL, so the same test
  ("every pack's licence text says CC0") means the same thing for both kinds of source.
- **The surveyed pool is recorded, not archived.** 20 candidates were fetched; one ships. The
  other 19 live in `sources/bigsoundbank/fetched.json` as id + title + page + sha256, re-fetchable
  without keeping their bytes in the repo.

### The finding inside the take

The useful whooshes are not the one-second files. `whoosh_s0572.ogg` is an **11-second mono take
holding eleven discrete sword swings**, and measured across all eleven they are far more
homogeneous than any four separate files could be: centroid 1433–1806 Hz, −40 dB extent
124–153 ms. That is exactly what a variant SET wants — four takes of one action, not four
different actions — so `process_reaction.py` grew the ability to take a REGION of a source. The
windows are written down as measured times rather than found by an onset detector, because a
detector in a shipping driver is a second thing that can drift; the source's `source_sha256` is
what keeps the numbers meaningful.

### The measurement that reversed a pick, and the one it discredits

`hurt` took three candidate sets before it was right, and the first two were rejected by a metric
this project had not used before.

The obvious pick is a body impact — `impactPunch_heavy`, because a punch is what "you took it"
sounds like — and the obvious way to choose among them is **spectral centroid**, which is what
every other cue in `art/audio/README.md` is chosen on. Both were wrong. Band-limit each candidate
to **500–4000 Hz**, the range a phone speaker actually reproduces, and take its RMS:

| | 500–4000 Hz RMS |
|---|---|
| the shipped `impact` set (the reference) | −38.7 … −39.7 dBFS |
| `impactPunch_heavy` (rejected twice) | **−48 … −57 dBFS** |
| `impactGlass_light` (shipped) | −32.5 … −33.1 dBFS |

96–98 % of the punches' energy sits below 300 Hz. On the WeChat target the game's single most
important feedback cue would have been **inaudible** while the cue it layers under was not — and
the centroid actively hid it, reading 594–873 Hz because a sparse high tail pulls it up over a
spectrum that is essentially all sub-bass.

**Centroid describes where a spectrum is centred. It says nothing about whether the listener's
speaker reaches it.** For any cue that has to survive a phone, measure the band.

The set that shipped closes a vocabulary on the way past: `shield.break` is already
`impactGlass_heavy`, so the character's shell now has **one material at two severities** — a light
glass tick when it is hit, a heavy glass shatter when it fails — while `deflect` stays metal,
which is what keeps a parry distinct from being hit.

### Where the peak-match reference comes from, and the test that keeps it true

`process_all.py` (the 2026-08-27 combat set) peak-matches each file to a re-rendered `synth.json`,
a scratch artefact that is not in the repo — so that driver is not re-runnable as written. Both
later drivers avoid it the same way `process_ui.py` first did: every voice they match against is a
**single `tone()`**, whose envelope ramps 0 → `gain` → 0 over a unit-amplitude oscillator, so its
delivered peak IS its `gain` argument, exactly. The reference is read off `platform/audioSynth.ts`
and moves only when that table moves.

That is a cross-language invariant with nothing holding it, so it now has a test.
`audioSynth.test.ts` asserts, for all eight sample-first cues (these four plus the four `ui.*`),
that the voice creates exactly one oscillator, no noise burst, and a first envelope ramp equal to
the exact number the Python driver used. Adding a second primitive to one of those voices would
leave a shipped file peak-matched to a level its own voice no longer produces, and every symptom
of that is a mix nobody can point at.

### The cap did not move, and that is measured too

The cue count went 20 → 24 and `CueMixer.DEFAULT_CAP` stayed at 12. It is a device budget, not
headroom over the cue list. What the extra cues cost is asserted rather than assumed: a frame
containing every engine cue overflows by exactly `sampledCues − cap` voices, and every sacrificed
voice ranks strictly below every survivor. `audioPipeline.test.ts` derives both numbers now — it
used to write them down (`expect(stolen.size).toBe(3)`), and they went stale the moment the set
grew. Four other files carried the same shape of literal (46 → 50 → 61) and were converted with
it; in every case the exact-set assertion beside them is what actually pins which files, so
nothing was lost.

## The victory sting stops playing over defeat screens (2026-09-02, client only, no engine bump)

The defect the pass above surfaced and deliberately left. **No new asset, no catalogue change, no
engine change** — one two-line branch became a question, plus a new 33-line module and nine tests.

### What was wrong

`EventReactor`'s `case 'win'` was two lines: `cue('win'); break;`. The engine's `win` event
(`engine/state/events.ts`) carries `winner: Winner` — a seat index, `'enemies'`, or null — and it
fires **once for the match**, when *anyone* wins. Three producers push it: `WinConditionSystem`'s
PvE wipe (`'enemies'`) and wave-clear (`0`), `ExtractionSystem`'s EXTRACT (`0`), and
`WinConditionSystem.tickPlacement`'s arena finish (the winning squad's lowest seat).

So the jingle was right in exactly one case — single-player PvE — and wrong in every PvP match.
Reproduced on the live `?arenaDemo=1` harness while verifying `death.player`: downing the local
seat and letting it bleed out produced `death.player:1` followed by `win:1` **in the same frame**,
with `phase` already `defeat` and ELIMINATED on screen.

### The fix, and why it is not a new `if`

The screen half of this question was already solved. `RunOutcome.handle` branched on
`s.zoneEnabled` and then on the winner, and its arena branch carried a comment explaining that
`s.winner` names a *representative* seat of the winning squad, so the test has to be team
membership. That is the same question the cue needs, so the condition moved out rather than being
written a second time:

- **`client/src/game/controllers/localOutcome.ts`** (new, 33 lines) — `localSeatWon(state,
  localOwner, winner)`. PvE: anything but `'enemies'` is the whole party's win, deliberately not
  matched against the `winner: 0` those producers hardcode (that literal is *"single-player: player
  id 0"*, not an owner). PvP: compare `teamId`s.
- **`RunOutcome.handle`** now computes `won` once from it and uses the arena/PvE split only to pick
  the *copy* — placement text or floor/materials text. Behaviour is unchanged; its 16 existing
  cases, including the 2026-08-04 squad-membership one, pass untouched.
- **`EventReactor`'s `case 'win'`** reads the same function with the winner the EVENT announced.
  Passing the winner rather than reading `state.winner` inside is deliberate: every producer sets
  the field and pushes the event in one tick, and a parameter makes that an argument instead of an
  assumption.

The reason to share rather than re-derive is concrete, not stylistic: comparing seat identity
instead of team membership was a **real bug on the screen side** — most of a winning squad saw
DEFEAT, fixed 2026-08-04 — and a cue with its own copy of the rule would have been free to make it
again, silently, in a mode nobody plays locally.

### A defeat does not get a cue of its own

It plays **`death.player`**, and the vocabulary was already built for this: design/11 authored that
file as the counterpart of `win` — same instrument, a descending scale against the jingle's own
figure — and ranked it at 118, directly under `win`'s 120, stealable by nothing else. Three things
follow:

- **It covers a case nothing else did.** A single-player PvE wipe ends the run the tick the sole
  player goes **down**, not dead, and `downed`'s only cue is `hurt`. Until this event arrives,
  nothing has said the fall was final — which is exactly why `downed` itself must not play
  `death.player` (a co-op revive is still possible up to that point), and why the `win` event is
  the right place for it.
- **The seat's own death and the match ending are ONE voice.** Where both land in the same frame,
  the existing coalescing makes it `death.player:2` — one voice at +15% gain (`coalesceBoost`), the
  same call `downed` already makes with the `hit` that caused it. Two sounds narrating one moment
  would be the bug's twin, not its fix.
- **No state, no guess.** With `activeState()` null — a menu frame draining a stale queue —
  *neither* cue plays: the jingle would be the original defect, and the fall would invent a defeat.
  Same silence the `hurt`/`death.player` local-seat gate already takes when there is no seat.

### Verified

**25 new cases across three test files** (4380 → **4405 client tests**) — `EventReactor.test.ts`
+9, `localOutcome.test.ts` 14 (new), `audioPipeline.test.ts` +2 — one layer per failure mode the
fix actually has.

**1. The cue picks the right branch** — `EventReactor.test.ts`'s "the cues that are about YOU"

**2. The shared rule is right** — `localOutcome.test.ts`, new, 14 cases. The edges neither caller

## Standing next to loot stops disarming the player (2026-09-02, client only, no engine bump)

A live report, in full: *"when there is a weapon nearby to pick up, do not block the player from attacking"* — when there is a weapon
you could pick up nearby, do not block the player's attack. **No engine change, no asset**: one
line moved out of `GameLoop`, one new latch on `CommandBuilder`, one listener on the panel.

### What was wrong

`ui/WeaponPickupPrompt.ts` opens whenever any `weapon`-kind pickup sits within
`SIM.lootRevealRadius` of the local seat, and `GameLoop.updateHud` gated the whole fire button on
it:

```ts
this.deps.builder.suppressFire(this.deps.portalPrompt.isOpen || this.deps.hud.weaponPickupPrompt.isOpen);
```

The reason that line exists is real: `WebInput` sets `firing` from a raw
`canvas.addEventListener('mousedown', …)`, which runs no matter what a Pixi button's hit test
consumed, so a click on a row would otherwise *also* fire a shot. The portal popup (`10`) solved
it the same way in 2026-08-02 and the pickup panel was OR'd into that call when it shipped
(`ENGINE_VERSION` 32).

What was not noticed is that the two panels live in completely different moments. The portal popup
only exists in a cleared room, so gating fire on it costs nothing. The pickup panel opens
**mid-fight, from `lootRevealRadius` away, every time anything drops a weapon** — which is every
kill. So the player stood in their own loot, held fire, and nothing happened, for as long as
anything remained on the floor near them. The panel had to be closed by hand (its `×`) to shoot,
and the next drop reopened it.

### The fix: swallow the press, not the panel

One press, not one panel, is what a UI click costs now.

- **`ui/WeaponPickupPrompt.ts`** — the panel container becomes `eventMode = 'static'` (so Pixi
  will notify a listener on it at all) and registers `pointerdowncapture`. **Capture phase, and
  that is the whole trick**: `Button` stops `pointerdown` from bubbling (`ui/widgets.ts`, so a
  button nested in a tap-anywhere screen does not double-fire), so a bubble-phase listener would
  never see a press that landed on a row — the exact presses this has to catch. Capture runs
  top-down before the target is notified, so it sees them all, and at `AT_TARGET` Pixi notifies
  the capture listener too, which is what catches a press on the chrome between rows.
- **`controllers/CommandBuilder.ts`** — `suppressFireUntilRelease()` sets a latch that drops the
  `FIRE` bit until the input reports not-firing, then clears itself. It is set from the panel's
  capture-phase `pointerdown`, which the browser dispatches **before** `mousedown` — so the latch
  is already up by the time `WebInput` has even heard about the click, and there is no matching
  "off" call to forget and no `pointerup` listener to keep in sync. `suppressFire(active)` stays
  exactly as it was for the portal popup.
- **`Game.ts`** — one wiring line beside the existing `weaponPickupPrompt.onPick` one.

A press already in flight when the panel opens is untouched, which is the point: holding fire
through a fight does not stop because a weapon dropped in range.

### Two Pixi facts this pass had to establish, both now written down in tests

- **`Text` labels are out of hit-testing** (`widgets.ts`, and the panel's own title). Pixi's own
  advice for non-interactive children, and here also load-bearing for the suite:
  `Text.containsPoint` measures its glyphs, measuring needs a real canvas, and the headless suite
  has none — so any `Text` under the press point threw before a hit test could resolve.
- **The events *mixin* is not installed in a headless test.** `addEventListener(…, {capture:true})`
  arrives with `environment-browser`'s mixin, which only runs once a browser `Application`
  initialises; the panel registers through `on('pointerdowncapture')` instead, which is the same
  registration (`FederatedEventTarget` maps one to the other) on the always-present EventEmitter.
  The test installs the mixin by hand — what `Application.init()` would have done — before
  driving a real `EventBoundary`.

### What is verified

- **`WeaponPickupPrompt.test.ts`** (+3): a press dispatched at a row reaches the panel *through
  `Button`'s `stopPropagation`*; a press hit-tested onto the chrome between rows reaches it; a
  press out in the world does not. The first case dispatches at the row rather than aiming at it,
  because nothing headless updates a world transform — an earlier version aimed at the row's
  screen position, silently resolved to the panel background, and would have passed in either
  phase. Both mutations (bubble instead of capture, `passive` instead of `static`) fail these.
- **`CommandBuilder.test.ts`** (+3): the latch swallows the press it lands in for as long as it is
  held, the next press fires normally with nothing turning it back off, and a press that never
  touched the panel fires while the panel is on screen.
- **`GameLoop.test.ts`** (+2): with the pickup panel reporting `isOpen`, `suppressFire` is called
  with `false`; with the portal popup open it is still called with `true`.
- **`gamePickupPanel.test.ts`** (new, 2 cases) — the WIRING, end to end through a real `Game`,
  in the shape `gameWeaponSwap.test.ts` established: a run is started, a weapon is dropped under
  the player's feet, and the assertions are what a player sees. Holding fire with the panel open
  puts **projectiles in the air and leaves the weapon on the floor**; a press on the panel
  followed by a tap on the row **collects the weapon and fires nothing**. Both `Game.ts` wiring
  lines are load-bearing here — deleting either one, or restoring the `|| isOpen` in `GameLoop`,
  fails one of these two cases. This is the coverage the 2026-08-04 entry declined to write
  ("`Game.ts`'s own two-line wiring stays untested... no test file for `Game.ts` exists
  anywhere"), the day after the panel shipped with `onPick` connected to nothing; that exemption
  has been obsolete since `installFakeTextCanvas` + a fake app made `Game` constructible under
  plain vitest, and this is the same wiring, one callback wider.
- **`widgets.test.ts`** (+1) and **`CommandBuilder.test.ts`** (+1, a second round): a `Button`
  takes a press on its box and never on its label — the half of `eventMode: 'none'` that must
  keep working, since a hole in the middle of every button would be the cost of getting it wrong
  — and the fire latch is FIRE-only, leaving `INTERACT` (a revive channel held through the click)
  and the one-shot latches on the same press untouched. Plus **`WeaponPickupPrompt.test.ts`**
  (+1): a closed panel swallows nothing, so dismissing it cannot trade a disarmed player for an
  invisible rectangle that eats attacks.
- **Live, on the dev server** (`beginRun()` + hand-driven `update(16)`, since rAF is throttled
  while the pane is hidden): with the panel open over a weapon at the player's feet, a raw
  `mousedown` in the world produced **3 projectiles** and left the pickup on the floor. A real
  trusted click on the row logged `press` then `pick:10` — the suppression latch and the collect,
  in that order, from one click.

`npm run check` green across every workspace (client 4405 -> 4418 tests, +13). `Game.ts`'s baseline entry moves 1132 → 1135 with
its reason recorded in `client/scripts/file-length-baseline.json`, the same
screen-callback-wiring category as the `hud.onSwapWeapon` and `hud.onSaveReplay` entries before it.

## The swing that was one frame long, because the field that said otherwise was never read (2026-09-02, engine + client, `ENGINE_VERSION` 52→53)

Not a report — a code question, and one the docs had already answered. `MeleeSpec.swingSec` was
authored on all seven melee weapons (0.1 s on the spear, 0.2 s on the hammer), carried the doc
comment *"ACTIVE hit-window (subset of cooldownSec), 07 step 7"*, and had a row in design/09's
conversion table: `swingSec → swingTicks (active-hit window, 07)`. `toSimSpec` never converted it.
`MeleeSimSpec` had no field to convert it into. **Nothing in the repo read it.**
`HitResolveSystem.meleeArc` resolved the whole arc on the single tick `justSwung` latched.

So the choice was: implement the window, or delete the field as dead authored data. That is not
actually a choice, because design/07 step 7 has specified the answer since it was written —
*"a swing is active for a few ticks (`swingTicks`); during active frames, test the weapon's arc …
each target is hit at most once per swing (track hit ids on the swing)"*. The field was
unimplemented spec, not dead data. Same shape as `MeleeSpec.knockback` (authored since Stage C,
wired in v25) and `RangedSpec.piercing` (v28) before it — the third time this exact
authored-but-unconverted gap has been found in `toSimSpec`.

### Why nothing caught it

Every melee test in the engine staged a swing by hand-latching `w.justSwung = true` and asserting
the hits on that same tick — which is exactly what a one-tick window produces. 1104 tests were
green over a mechanic that did not exist. The hammer's 0.2 s and the spear's 0.1 s were
identically timed, and no assertion anywhere could tell.

The render layer had the same shape of hole from the other end. `rigAttackMotion`'s `SWING_MS` was
a hardcoded 260 ms, chosen — its own comment said so — as a fraction of the STARTER SABER's
recovery. Every blade in the roster animated on one weapon's clock, and none of them animated on
the sim's hit window, because there wasn't one.

### What shipped

`MeleeSimSpec.swingTicks` (converted, clamped into `[1, swingCooldownTicks]` — a `swingSec` that
rounds to 0 would silently disarm a weapon, and design/07 authors the window as a *subset* of the
recovery) plus three `WeaponState` fields: `swingTicksLeft`, `swingHitIds`, `swingDamage`.
`WeaponFireSystem` counts the window down at step 3 alongside the cooldown; `HitResolveSystem` and
`DeflectSystem` both gate on `swingTicksLeft > 0` instead of `justSwung`, which survives as the
one-tick START latch that freezes the payload and emits `melee_swing`.

The arc re-tests against the **live** facing and positions each active tick — that is the sweep: a
target that walks in is caught, and a player who turns through their own swing carries the arc
with them. The payload does not re-test: damage and the crit roll freeze on the start tick into
`swingDamage`, and `swingHitIds` caps each body at one hit for the whole swing. Both are
design/07's "one frozen payload"; without them a 6-tick hammer would deal its damage six times and
draw `combatPrng` six times for one attack.

`openSwing`/`closeSwing` (`content/weapons.ts`) are the single definition of the transition, added
because a caller that set `justSwung` without the window — or the reverse — produces a swing that
silently does nothing, and six test setups were about to be exactly that caller. `ApplyInputSystem`
closes the window on a weapon swap: only the active weapon is ticked, so an open window on a
holstered blade would freeze mid-swing and re-open, still armed, whenever the player swapped back.

**Deflect widened too, deliberately.** design/07's locked decision is *"the SAME arc both damages
enemies and deflects bullets — there is no separate `blockArc`"*, and an arc that damages for four
ticks but parries for one is a separate blockArc in all but name. The parry window went from one
tick to 3-6 by weapon. That is a real balance move on the pivot mechanic, not a free refactor, and
it is called out as one in design/07 — it stands because the commitment design/05 asks for is
spending the swing, and frame-perfect parry timing was not readable at 30 Hz. Watch it in playtest;
the lever is now `swingSec` per weapon, which is a real number for the first time.

### What the golden gate said, measured before the bump

The measurement only means anything before bumping — `version` is serialized into the hashed state,
so the bump alone moves all five scenario hashes:

```
                       deflect        hit        melee_swing   prngCursors
ember-dungeon-floor1     5 → 7      62 → 61          67 → 67      unchanged
launch-arena-pvp         1 → 3      51 → 53          44 → 44      unchanged
arena-waves / walls-and-pillars / brim-grinder: hash moved, witness unchanged
```

Three separate claims, each a thing that could have gone wrong. **`deflect` up** — the wider parry
catches bullets the one-tick window missed, in shipped content. **`melee_swing` unchanged** — the
swing count did not move, so the window did not become a second trigger. **`prngCursors`
unchanged** — the load-bearing one: the crit is still exactly one draw per swing, not one per
active tick. A per-tick re-roll would have moved that column in every scenario.

### Render — and a concurrent session that had already built half of it

The task's framing named `client/src/game/fx/slashArc.ts` and a `swingSchedule` in
`rigAttackMotion.ts`. Neither existed at this branch's base commit, and both existed by the time
the engine half was done: **another session landed
[the swing shows the sector it actually hits](15-2026-09-02-melee-sector.md#the-swing-shows-the-sector-it-actually-hits-2026-09-02-client-only-no-engine-bump)
(`c25edea`) in parallel**, which is what the framing was written against. It built the exact chain
this branch's client half was independently building — `SwingShape` threaded through
`onAttack`/`attack`/`kick`, a derived `SwingSchedule` — plus the sector fx on top.

Their payload was richer than mine (it also sizes the SWEEP off `arcDeg`, and drives the fx off
`strikeStartMs`/`strikeEndMs` so the blade in the air and the light on the ground are one event),
so this branch's superseded chain edits were **dropped before the merge** rather than resolved
hunk by hunk. The engine half they never touched.

What was left to do is precisely what the task asked for, and their own file said so twice:
`swingSchedule` timed off **`recoveryMs`** — the 11-tick gap until you may swing *again* — with a
comment conceding it as the reference it had. It was the only number available: `swingSec` was
unconverted and the sim had no window at all. So `SwingShape.recoveryMs` became
`SwingShape.windowMs`, sourced from `swingTicks`, anchored so `strikeEndMs` lands on the window's
close. The saber moves 260 → ~242 ms, the hammer to ~364, the spear to ~182, and the sector fx
inherits all of it for free. The two inputs are not even proportional across the roster — the
spear spends 33% of its recovery active, the frostbrand 36% — so no constant could have stood in,
and a test now pins that ratio specifically so a silent revert to `recoveryMs` cannot pass.

The wind-up still plays inside the live window, which is the one part that cannot be honest: the
render layer learns about a swing on the tick it starts and cannot anticipate it.

**One field was deleted rather than shipped.** An earlier draft put `swingTicks` on the
`melee_swing` event. Their `EventReactor.meleeSwinger` already resolves the whole spec out of the
`GameState` every client holds — deliberately, with a comment saying so — which left my field with
no reader at all. Shipping it would have created a second source of truth for a number its only
consumer already had: the same authored-but-unread shape `swingSec` spent two months in, in the
same pass that removed it. Removed, with the reasoning recorded on the event type itself.

### Tests

`engine/systems/meleeWindow.test.ts` is new — **33 tests**, written against one specific failure
mode: a window test that would also pass with `swingTicks: 1`. So each one either counts the active
ticks it observed, or proves the target was unreachable on the swing tick before asserting it was
reached later. Covered: the per-weapon conversion and both clamps; the window being exactly
`swingTicks` long and a hammer's really being longer than a spear's; one hit per body across the
whole window and the hit list not leaking into the next swing; one `combatPrng` draw per window and
none at all for a zero-crit build; a target walking in mid-swing, a swinger turning onto one, and
both edges (nothing before the window, nothing after); deflect on a later active tick and not after
the close; a player downed or killed mid-swing; a blade holstered mid-swing.

A second pass added the three things the first could not see, each with its own history of going
wrong here:

- **The real `step()` order.** Hand-driving `fire.tick(s); hits.tick(s)` proves the two systems
  agree with each other, not that the other eleven leave the window alone — `ENGINE_VERSION` 49
  found a bug of exactly that shape. Three tests now drive `GameEngine.step()` with a swap-then-
  hold-FIRE stream and pin the whole trace: swings at ticks 2/13/24, active ticks
  `[2,3,4,5, 13,14,15,16, 24,25,26,27]`, and an enemy parked in the arc sitting at 1 hp for all
  four ticks of the first window before the second one kills it.
- **Whether the new fields are actually in the replay hash.** `replay.ts`'s own comment says a
  golden-replay comparison of two identical runs "never breaks" on a new always-equal field — so
  adding a line to `serializeState` is untested by construction. Four tests perturb one field each
  on one of two byte-equal engines.
- **The `openSwing`/`closeSwing` contracts** no caller happens to exercise (the ranged no-op, the
  clear-don't-reallocate hit list), plus an invariant test proving the restart-mid-window branch is
  **unreachable in shipped content**: no melee weapon's window survives its own cooldown even at
  `BUFF_CAPS.mult_firerate`. It fails the day someone raises that cap, which is exactly when the
  branch stops being theoretical.

Plus 8 in `rigAttackMotion.test.ts`, 1 in `RigSkin.test.ts` and 1 in `Skin.test.ts` for the render
