## Twice the chest, and the size nothing was watching (2026-09-21, client + test + docs, no engine change)

> *"宝箱的体积放大一倍"* — with a screenshot of a floor whose chest sits under the portal,
> reading as litter on the tiles rather than as the thing the room is named after.

One constant: `ChestLayer`'s `BODY_HALF` 9/14 → **18/28**, so the drawn footprint goes 18 px → 36 px
for a small chest and 28 px → 56 px for a big one. Everything else follows it without being told —
the sprite is scaled by WIDTH with the art's own aspect setting its height, the Graphics fallback is
fitted to the same width so a late texture cannot make the chest jump, and the ground shadow is an
ellipse on the same half-width. The screenshot's complaint is really about a comparison the constant
never made: a player's drawn body is 32 px, so the small chest was **nine px of half-width beside a
sixteen** — furniture smaller than the character who loots it.

**Two things deliberately did not move.** `PLATE_RADIUS_PX` is drawn to the sim's own
`CHEST_MECHANISM_RADIUS_GRID`, which is the whole reason a player can answer "am I standing on it"
by looking; scaling it with the body would make the picture lie about where the mechanic measures.
And nothing in the engine changed at all — a chest is not a collider, so the drawn box has never
been anything an actor can walk into, and `CHEST_OPEN_RANGE_GRID` is measured from the chest's
centre rather than from its edge.

### 1,509 tests passed a doubling without one going red

That is not a hole anyone left open by accident — it is what the assertions were *built* to do.
Every size expectation in `ChestLayer.test.ts` and `chestArt.test.ts` derives from
`chestFootprintWidth()` rather than restating 18 or 28, which is the right way to write them (a
restated literal tests a constant against itself) and is exactly why they all survive a change to
that function's answer. What is left is a size pinned from **below** — `big > small`, design/13's
dual-channel rule — and from nowhere at all above. Any width whatsoever was legal.

So the ceiling is now stated, twice, as a RELATION against the sim's own numbers rather than as a
size somebody picked:

- **A chest is never drawn wider than the range that opens it.** Past that, the art reaches further
  than the mechanic does, and a player standing squarely on the box — the most explicit *I am here*
  a player can perform — is out of range of the thing they are standing on. Half-width 28 against
  48 px of open range: the shipped big chest has room, and one more doubling does not.
- **A big chest stays clear of its own mechanism plates.** `config.ts` gives the ring radius a
  reason in words — *"wide enough that standing on one plate is visibly NOT standing on the
  chest"* — and that sentence is about a drawn width the config file cannot see. The near edge of a
  plate is at `(RING − RADIUS)` = 64 px; the body ends at 28.

Both were mutation-checked rather than assumed, because a ceiling a long way above the value it
guards is indistinguishable from no ceiling: doubling again (36/56) turns the first one red, and
65 turns both red. `ChestLayer.test.ts` 23 → **25** cases, all green, plus the 30 across the two
chest files and the 1,509 in `game/scene`. No production behaviour outside the drawn width, no
`ENGINE_VERSION` bump, no golden re-record.
