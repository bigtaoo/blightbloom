## A door has a clock, and direction is what says "you can walk through this" (2026-09-03, client only, no engine bump)

Live report, with a screenshot of a doorway: *"I want effects on the doors that tell passable from blocked. The current form is too rigid"*
— put fx on the doors that distinguish passable from blocked, the current form is too rigid.

**Three earlier passes had already tried, and the diagnosis is why they could not work.** The
2026-08-30 through/spill/rim lighting, the 2026-08-30b floor tile and illustrated curtain, and
volume 16's single door height each added more layers to this fixture. Every one of them added a
STILL layer — drawn once in `buildDoorBlock` and thereafter only toggled by `.visible`. The
report was not "the cue is weak": **nothing in this project could animate a scene fixture at
all.** `Scene.interpolate` walks `Scene.views`, which holds actors, bullets and pickups; a door
is added straight to `layers.entities` by `RoomBuilder` and is in no such list. Measured on a
live frame, two `renderer.extract` grabs 480 ms apart over the leaf's own bounds: **mean 0.01
luma, 0.2% of pixels moving more than 3/255**, against a control patch of plain floor at exactly
0. A still image, confirmed as one rather than assumed.

**The same gap had quietly frozen the portal for three weeks.** `Portal.interpolate` — an alpha
pulse, two counter-rotating rings and ten infalling motes, written 2026-08-12 and described in
`design/12` as the reason the arch is "a split, not a sprite" — **had no caller anywhere in the
repo**. It drew one frame of a spinning vortex and held it. `RoomBuilder.tickFixtures` now drives
both, off the `dt` `GameLoop.updateFx` already holds and from the one wrapper every render path
already calls.

### The clock is spent on direction, not on wobble

A still image speaks with COLOUR and SHAPE, and both were already committed — the two states
share one floor-pool shape on purpose and differ by hue. Motion adds three channels:

- **Direction** carries the read. A LOCKED door's motion is CONTAINED: flame scrolls upward
  inside the leaf, a scan bar ping-pongs between the jambs, its floor ring travels INWARD.
  Nothing crosses the threshold. An OPEN door's motion CROSSES it: light streams down and out,
  motes drift onto the floor toward the player, its floor ring travels OUTWARD. This matters
  more than it sounds — the shipped locked leaf is a red hazard panel and the shipped open
  curtain is a gold streaming one, and at a glance in a lava biome those are two warm rectangles.
- **Rhythm** carries the urgency. Locked is fast and restless (1.7 s and 2.75 s beating at a
  ratio that lands on no simple fraction, so the pair has no visible loop); open is one slow
  2.4 s breath that the curtain, the spill pool and the ramp all share.
- **Reaction** is the channel a still door could not have: proximity brightens a door as the
  player approaches, and a locked one flashes when they walk into it.

**The unlock is an event now.** A lock flip used to set six layers' `.visible` in one frame, at
the single most meaningful moment in a room. It crossfades over 350 ms (the outgoing side
squared, so the eye lands on the arriving state) with a second leaf sprite holding the outgoing
elevation, and throws off one ring — outward and warm on unlock, inward and red when a fight
seals a room.

**The refusal never reaches the sim.** Walking into a locked door flashes it and adds 0.05
trauma. A `door_blocked` event would be cleaner and costs an `ENGINE_VERSION` bump plus a
golden-hash re-run for something that changes no simulation state, so `doorTick.isRefused` reads
what the client already has: the door is locked, the player is within 20 px of the passage AND
pushing into it, and they are **not actually moving**. That last one is what separates "walked
into it and stopped" from "walking past it" — the sim has already resolved the collision, so a
blocked player's `cur` simply stops leaving `prev`. Debounced at 450 ms, and deliberately not
paired with `addHitStop`: freezing the sim over a navigation mistake punishes one.

### No new art, and why that was the cheaper answer

The owner's constraint, from experience: *"aian ordinary sprite-sheet sequence is hard to use directly"*. It is the same conclusion
`design/12` reached for the portal, and the answer is the same split — **the motion is generated,
not prompted.** Two seamless fields baked by `shadeRamp.bakedField` (zero bytes against
`design/04`'s package budget, POT, mipmappable, readable back by a test) scroll under the shipped
stills, which keep supplying the material an image model is actually good at. Nothing in this
pass adds a byte to any pack.

Two properties of those bakes are load-bearing and invisible when wrong:

- **Seamless in y** — every vertical term is a sine of an integer number of cycles over the tile.
  The first version was not: it carried a `0.35 + 0.65 * (1 - y/h)` "fire is densest low" bias,
  which is not periodic, and scrolled a hard seam up the fire once per 1.7 s. The test caught it
  before a frame did. That bias is a SCREEN-space property anyway — baked in it would travel with
  the scroll — so it moved to stacking the second flame layer over the band's lower 62%.
- **Faded at both x edges.** Over `w - 1`, not `w`: with `x / w` the last column lands at
  `sin(0.984π) = 0.05`, a 12/255 hairline down the right side and nothing down the left.

The flame overlay is the one layer that cannot let the art mask it (the hazard leaf is opaque),
so it is confined to a **measured** band — `FLAME_BAND`, x 0.197–0.803 and y 0.184–0.816 of
`door_locked_raw.png`, re-derived from the file's own pixels by `doorArtBands.test.ts` every run
(saturation × value, fire against desaturated stone, a plateau stable to ±0.01 across thresholds
0.3–0.4). The open state's streams need no such number: they sit behind the leaf and get the
arch's own stone as a mask for free.

### What it cost, and what it bought

Only doors whose footprint meets the visible world rect are stepped, grown by 96 px for a camera
rect that is one frame stale. Verified live on level 1 with one door on screen and four built:
**60 ticks in 60 frames for the visible one, 0 for the other three.** The whole pass measures
below the noise floor of a 120-frame timing (0.111 ms/frame with it on against 0.144 with it
suppressed — not distinguishable from run-to-run variance).

The only per-frame cost is the motes, a `Graphics` rebuilt each frame, and they ride the
`particleBudget` the quality profile already carries rather than a new tier field — the low
tier's 0.35 thins five to two, floored at one, because "things come OUT of here" is a legibility
cue and not decoration. Everything else is transform animation.

A/B on a live frame, the pass suppressed and then restored, same region both times:

| | mean luma delta over 480 ms | pixels moving > 3/255 |
|---|---|---|
| locked, before | 0.01 | 0.2% |
| locked, after | 4.11 | 30.3% |
| open, before | 0.56 (the player's own rig crossing the doorway) | 1.2% |
| open, after | 5.30 | 45.0% |

Confinement, measured the same way: inside the fire band the locked door moves by a mean of 7.30
with a max of 64/255; on the stone jamb 6 px to its left, a **max of 4/255** — the glow's own
ambient breath over the whole leaf, not the overlay leaking. Brightness is essentially unchanged
in both states (open 104.6 → 101.8, locked 71.7 → 73.8), which is the intent: the 2026-08-30
sweep had already settled what value a doorway may sit at, and this pass adds motion, not light.

**One defect the frame caught that no test would have.** Both floor rings were full ellipses
centred on the threshold, so their northern halves drew straight up the door's own stone — a 2 px
stroke at 0.3 alpha crossing the hazard leaf and the flanking wall, reading as a stray red line
through the masonry. They are half ellipses now, opening south onto the floor only. `GLOW_POOL`
gets away with a full ellipse because it is nine fills at 0.035; a stroke has nowhere to hide.

### Two things that had to be settled to make it safe

- **One writer per `alpha`.** `occlusion.fadeGroup` captures each layer's alpha once and then
  writes `base * fade`; `DoorFx` rewrites those same alphas every frame, and the fx pass runs
  AFTER the x-ray — so `DoorFx` would have won, silently disabling the fade on a door's own
  layers, one of which (`buildOpenFloorTile`'s tile) is fully opaque and would then hide the
  character standing in the doorway. That is the exact defect the x-ray exists to prevent. Those
  layers left the fade group and are represented in it by one `DoorFx.xrayLayer` proxy whose
  value the controller folds into everything it writes.
- **`pingPong` never worked.** `((t % p) + p) / p` only rescues a negative clock; it does not wrap
  a positive one, so it returned 1.5 at half a period and swept the bar off to `2 - 3 = -1`.

### The test pass, and the four survivors that were all the same shape

Asked for separately (*"are there tests worth adding"*) once the pass was working, which turned out to be the
right order: the gaps a battery finds in a rendering pass are not edge cases.

Four gaps were closed before any mutation ran, each a class this suite could not see: a
**shipped-content sweep** (`doorFxCoverage.test.ts`, the sibling of `doorStandCoverage` —
all 24 doors, both passage shapes, both mapping branches present, every band over half its own
leaf), the **`GameLoop` wiring** (the whole feature can be deleted and every module's own tests
stay green), **`RoomBuilder`** (including the portal, whose revival nothing had asserted), and the
two new accessors `CommandBuilder.lastMove` / `FxController.worldView`.

Then **70 mutants over the five files this pass touched, 70 killed** — but not on the first run.
55 rows scored 51, and **every one of the four survivors was a sentence this log makes that
nothing asserted**: the motes "accelerate out of the passage" (a linear fall passed every
existing assertion), the crossfade "clears the outgoing state early" (the suite could see that a
crossfade ran and nothing about its shape), the ghost "carries the art we left" (alpha and
visibility asserted, the TEXTURE not — so the transition would have crossfaded an empty sprite,
restoring the very cut it exists to remove). The fourth is the different verdict: the
degenerate-input guard is runtime-EQUIVALENT for height, and what it actually buys is a FINITE
coordinate, so the fix was to assert that rather than to add a redundant test.

A second battery ran over `doorLights.ts` — the file that only MOVED in this pass, whose evidence
was "every existing assertion still passes", the exact sentence the 2026-08-27 note warns about.
12 of 13, and the survivor **predates this pass by five weeks**: deleting the EAST jamb's rim band
left every spill assertion green, because they counted bands rather than sides. A doorway lit
down one side only is obvious in the room and was invisible to the suite. Both sides are asserted
band-for-band now.
