## A mob has one size while walking and a bigger one while standing (2026-09-03, engine, `ENGINE_VERSION` 55)

### The report is two rules, and they pull in opposite directions

The screenshot is the symptom; the sentence after it is the diagnosis, and it is a better one than
"mobs overlap". A mob carried ONE size, and that size was being asked two questions that want
different answers:

- **travelling**, its size has to be its body, or it cannot fit through a gap the level authored
  for it — the report's own example is a 1.5-body-wide slit, which one mob must walk through at
  full speed;
- **standing**, its size should be its personal space, which is much bigger — two mobs that have
  both stopped should end up four body radii apart, not touching.

With only the first, every mob in a room solves the identical problem — walk at the player, stop
at `engageRangeFp` — and therefore arrives at the identical place. `MovementSystem.resolveActorPairs`
does keep them from interpenetrating, but it is a COLLISION rule: it fires only once two feet
circles already overlap, and `footprintRadius` is 7 px against a 15 px body. "Not overlapping" by
that rule still means two sprites drawn almost exactly on top of each other, which is the
screenshot. v42's perception radius (mobs notice you at 320 px rather than room-wide) fixed the
approach reading as one converging column; it never touched the destination.

### What shipped

A mob's arrival became a piece of state, and the second radius hangs off it.

- **`EnemyActor.holding`** — written by `AIDecideSystem` every tick, true once the mob has reached
  its engage range and stopped to shoot. Not a latch like `aggroed`: a mob that loses its target
  or gets left behind is moving again and stops reserving space.
- **`standoffRadius(a)`** in `state/actorRadius.ts` — `STANDOFF_BODY_MULTIPLE` (2) × the body
  `radius`. That file is the "which radius answers which question" registry and now names four;
  this is the first one in it that is not a collision rule at all. The other three answer *may
  these two shapes overlap*, a constraint. This one answers *how far apart would two stopped mobs
  LIKE to stand*, a preference.
- **`MovementSystem.resolveStandingSpacing`** — a new pass between the collision pair push and the
  solid re-separation, which drifts two mobs apart to that distance, **but only when both are
  `holding`**.

That gate is the design, not a detail. A travelling mob neither exerts nor receives the push, so
the 1.5-body slit stays passable at full speed even with a mob standing at its mouth, and nothing
about routing, clearance or `solidRadius` moved at all. The obvious one-line version of this
feature — a bigger radius in `resolveActorPairs` — delivers the first half of the report and
breaks the second, which is precisely why the report spelled both out.

Three properties keep it a shuffle rather than a shove:

1. It is applied ON TOP of the collision push, never instead of it, and v49's solid re-separation
   still runs afterwards. Stone always wins; a mob backed into a corner just stops spreading.
2. Each mob's displacement is capped per tick at its own `moveSpeedPerTick` — a crowd unpacks over
   about half a second instead of exploding apart on one frame. The cap is per ACTOR rather than
   per pair, which is what stops the mob in the middle of a press from being launched by the sum
   of six neighbours.
3. The pushes are accumulated first and applied after, so the outcome does not depend on the order
   pairs are visited: determinism by construction rather than by pinning an iteration order.

`holding` also made "in engage range" hysteretic (`HOLD_RELEASE_PERMILLE`, 1.5×). The spacing push
moves a standing mob outward, so a mob that stopped exactly on the ring gets nudged just past it by
its neighbour — and under a bare threshold it would re-chase, be pushed out, re-chase, forever,
with its gun stuttering on and off behind it because fire slots follow the same range test.

### The balance cost, measured rather than assumed

`test:pve-sim`, spacing disabled vs enabled, widened from the shipped 8 seeds to 24 for the
measurement (the 8-seed gates all still pass on the shipped set):

| | extracted | avg floor reached | avg kills |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| careful bot, before | 13% | 2.5 | 155 |
| careful bot, after | **4%** | **1.5** | **104** |
| aggressive bot, before | 0% | 0.1 | 26 |
| aggressive bot, after | 0% | 0.1 | **17** |

So this made the game harder, and not in the way one would guess. Damage TAKEN per second barely
moved (worst 1 s window 6 either way, average 4.2 → 4.4): the volley is not heavier, the player
kills slower and therefore bleeds for longer. Two compounding reasons, both consequences of the
dispersal rather than defects in it — a spread arc is a crossfire where a blob is one bearing that
can be dodged as a unit; and `ROOM_FIRE_BUDGET` awards its two slots to the NEAREST contenders, so
a spread garrison rotates those slots as the player moves and a freshly-slotted mob can arrive with
a spent cooldown. That second one is visible in the golden fixture: ember floor 1 fires 173 → 186
bullets over the same 1500 ticks.

A mitigation was tried and measured wrong, which is the part worth recording. The theory was that
mobs settling further out (the 1.5× release) were the cause; tightening it to 1.1× took the careful
bot to **0% extraction, avg floor 1.2** — mobs holding their spacing closer to the player are
harder to survive, not easier. The difficulty is coming from the dispersal itself, i.e. from the
feature. Left as measured rather than compensated for: the dial is `ROOM_FIRE_BUDGET`, an
encounter-level number with its own tuning history, and moving it is a difficulty decision rather
than part of this change.

### Coverage, and the three things the battery found

`engine/systems/enemySpacing.test.ts`, 10 new, all driven through the real AIDecide + Movement pair
because every interesting failure is in the seam between them: the pair that settles at four body
radii and stays there; the five-deep press where no mob is displaced faster than it can walk
(started outside each other's feet circles, so the uncapped collision push cannot be what is being
measured); the traveller whose route past a stander is identical tick-for-tick to the same route
through an empty room; the 45 px gap walked through with a mob standing at its mouth; the player
who is never pushed; the mob nudged outside the ring that keeps holding AND keeps firing; the wall
that overrules the spread; the un-noticed garrison that keeps the spots the level authored for it;
the standoff stated as a NUMBER; and the pair that stops reserving space when the player dies under
it.

**The last three exist because of a mutation battery**, 15 mutants over the new pass, the `holding`
writes and the two balance constants — run with **`goldenHash` excluded on purpose**, since every
mutant moves a recorded hash and leaving it in reports ALL-KILLED while saying nothing about
whether the behavioural tests discriminate. First run: 12 killed, 3 survivors, three different
kinds of gap.

| survivor | what it meant |
| --- | --- |
| `pen = want` instead of `want - dist` — push the whole separation every tick | The settle assertion had px-scale slack. The real equilibrium is EXACT (`dist === want`, over by 0 fp) and the mutant lands ~1.8 px past it, just inside a 2 px tolerance. Pinned as the equality now, which is also the honest statement of what the pass does. |
| `holding` never cleared when the target is gone | Nothing had ever killed the player out from under a holding pair. Now something does. |
| `holding` never cleared for an un-noticed mob | **The line was dead.** `aggroed` is a one-way latch with a single writer and `holding` can only be set after it, so the branch can never see a holding mob. Deleted rather than tested (v46's precedent), with the golden gate staying green across the deletion as the evidence it was inert. |

A 16th — one mob absorbing the whole push instead of half each — was killed only *incidentally*, by
a distant hysteresis assertion, so the settle test now also pins the pair's MIDPOINT (free to move
by the 1 fp floor/remainder tie-break and no further). After the fixes: **15/15 killed.**

Three of the five golden scenarios diverged (`walls-and-pillars` and `brim-grinder` never get a mob
into engage range at all) — full hashes, witness deltas and the reasoning in
`ENGINE_VERSION_HISTORY.md`'s v55 entry.
