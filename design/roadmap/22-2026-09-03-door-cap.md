## Only the door at the door, and no wall hanging over it (2026-09-03, client only, no engine bump)

Live report, a screenshot of a shipped `128x64` kerb doorway with the mass above it circled in
red: *"at the door's position I want only the door, with no wall at either end of the opening"* — at the door's position I want only the door, no
wall at the entrance.

**The report named the symptom; the cause was the previous pass's own arithmetic.** Standing all
24 doors at one height fixed the letterbox opening and left a second artifact behind on exactly
the 11 it fixed. A door's **cap** is the wall over its lintel, and the reason it reads as stone is
stated in `design/01`'s own list: *"tiled from the same world-aligned swatch as the runs either
side, so a room's crown line runs unbroken THROUGH the doorway."* That sentence quietly assumes
the runs either side reach the cap. Through a 22 px kerb they do not — the doorway now out-tops
them by 82 px — so the cap came out as a full footprint depth of tiled wall swatch, 64 px of it,
sitting 82 px above the crown line on both sides with nothing under it. A slab of wall hanging in
mid-air over the opening.

Swept over the five shipped floors, all 11 kerb doorways had one; the 13 perimeter doorways never
did, because their flanks really are `WALL_H_PERIMETER` and their cap really is the continuation
it claims to be.

**So the cap became conditional on the thing it was always claiming.** `wallRuns.doorFlankHeight`
measures the SHORTEST run abutting the passage — the deleted `doorFlankTier`'s predicate, restored
for a different question, and shortest for the same reason it was shortest then: a cap resting on
one flank's crown while floating over the other is still a floating slab. `doorCapless` is the
production rule on top of it, and `RoomBuilder.buildDoors` folds the answer into the door's own
joins.

`WallJoins.capless` is a caller-set flag exactly like `doorClip`, and it is read in exactly one
place — `blockCapTop`. That is the point of putting it there rather than in the renderer: every
cap-shaped cue in this layer already derives its extent from that one function, so one flag drops
`addCapLayers`, the cap depth gradient, the cap edge bevel and the coping **together**, instead of
four call sites agreeing by hand and drifting later. The fixture's topmost row becomes the top of
its own arch, which is what an archway standing in a low wall looks like, and the occlusion
occluder follows for free (its `top` is that same `blockCapTop`), so the x-ray stops reserving a
band of floor for stone that is no longer drawn.

**The height is untouched, and so is every perimeter doorway.** This is not a partial revert of
`DOOR_H`: all 24 doors still stand at one height and the 13 through a room boundary draw the cap
they always have. What varies is whether there is stone above the lintel at all — a property of
the WALL, not of the door.

### What was measured, and where

Not a screenshot. The dev server was driven from the console to the shipped `forge → extraction`
floor and every built fixture read back:

| passage | `capLayers` | topmost row (local y) |
|---|---|---|
| `64x128` x3 — through a room boundary | 1 | −211 (unchanged) |
| `128x64` — through the kerb boundary | **0** | **−105** (was −169) |

−105 is `-DOOR_H` plus the silhouette stroke: the fixture paints nothing above its own arch.

Three test files carry the claim, and the split between them is deliberate:

- **`doorStandCoverage.test.ts`** sweeps the five shipped floors through `RoomBuilder.buildDoors`'
  own sequence and pins the 11/13 split — through `doorCapless` itself, not a second copy of the
  rule that could agree with this document and disagree with the renderer. The test it replaced
  asserted the opposite (*"its cap is the full footprint depth above the opening, unclipped"*) and
  would have gone on passing, because it computed the joins itself and never saw `capless`.
- **`RoomBuilder.test.ts`** asserts it on a really-built fixture at both flank heights. `capLayers`
  is precisely the group `addCapLayers` fills, so an empty one is the absence of the slab rather
  than a proxy for it — and the perimeter case is asserted non-empty in the same pass, so
  "capless" cannot be satisfied by breaking every door's cap.
- **`wallRuns.test.ts`** covers the pure halves: `capless` beating both other cap clips, and
  `doorFlankHeight` taking the shorter of two flanks, reading both jamb axes, and refusing a wall
  that only kisses the doorway at a corner (without that overlap test a corner-touching kerb would
  uncap a perimeter door).

### What it cost

`RoomBuilder.ts` was at 499 lines — one under the convention — and this pass pushed it to 510.
Rather than baseline it, the rule moved into `wallRuns.ts` where its siblings already live
(CLAUDE.md form ①), and `buildDoors`' six-line inline `skin` parameter type became
`Omit<DoorSkin, 'leaf'>`, which is literally what it always was. 498 lines, gate green, and the
file is shorter than it was before the pass.

**Files:** `scene/wallRuns.ts` (`WallJoins.capless`, `blockCapTop`, `doorFlankHeight`,
`doorCapless`), `scene/doorRender.ts` (the one guard), `scene/wallShadingSurfaces.ts` (two
degenerate-cap early returns), `scene/RoomBuilder.ts`. Four test files; **4,835 client tests
green**, `tsc --noEmit` clean, file-length gate clean.
