## The PvP bot parries (2026-10-03, client + server + tools + test + docs, no ENGINE_VERSION change)

The owner picked the second open item from the 2026-10-03 status review: the shipped arena bot
never parried. Every loadout carries a deflecting blade (ENGINE_VERSION 45), and a swing bats
back any hostile bullet in its arc. But `PvpBotController` never left its gun, so no match, sim
or real, ever had a parry in it. `PVP_DEFLECT_DAMAGE_PERMILLE` (a rival's rebound at half damage)
had never come into play. Only the sim-only `ArenaBotController` parried (volume 117), and it
does so frame-perfectly.

### The rule

`client/src/game/controllers/ai/parry.ts`, layered over the bot's command in
`PvpBotController.build`:

- **Swap in and swing.** When a picked bullet will be inside the blade's reach within
  `PARRY_LOOKAHEAD` = 4 ticks, the bot swaps to the blade and swings on the same tick. The
  bullet must also be inside the swing's arc, which faces the nearest hostile. The blade must be
  ready: a holstered weapon's cooldown is frozen, so a recovering blade could not turn the
  bullet.
- **Swing at a body, then swap back.** With the blade out and nothing coming, it swings only at
  a body inside the blade's reach. It swaps back to the gun once the blade has recovered.
- **What it skips.** It ignores rebounds (a bullet turns back once, ENGINE_VERSION 85), still
  bullets, and bullets moving away.
- **One bullet in two.** It picks `PARRY_SHARE_PERCENT` = 50% of bullets, by a hash of the
  bullet id and the seat. The pick holds for the bullet's whole flight, and two seats do not
  pick alike. A bot that turned every shot back would be a wall for someone learning the game.
  This number is a guess for playtest to settle.
- **No memory.** The bot stays a pure function of state. It presses swap only when last tick's
  buttons did not hold it (`PlayerActor.prevButtons`, the engine's own press edge).
- **Not while reviving.** A command holding INTERACT is left alone, because the revive channel
  is the commitment.

The sim bot switches this off in its base (`parries: false`) and keeps its own frame-perfect rule
behind its `parries` flag. Its `shipped` profile still means the gun-only bot every capacity
sweep measured.

### Found on the way: the server bot read the match three frames late

`BotClient` built its `CoopSession` with the default jitter cushion of 3 frames. The game's own
clients have run at 0 since volume 119 (`onlineConnect.ts`), because a cushion only smooths what
a player sees, and a bot sees nothing. For a parry, the cost was large. Over three duels at the
shipped 3 frames per batch, the bot parried 30 times offline, 26 times online at 0, and 11 times
online at 3. At 3 it was also hit 17 times, against 10 at 0. `BOT_BUFFER_FRAMES` = 0 now.

Leading the look-ahead by the online delay changed nothing. A lead of 0 to 4 extra ticks parried
the same 8-9 per duel, so there is no lead.

### Measured

`test:pvp-sim`, same 180 matches, parry off (before) and on (after):

| | parries | rebounds landed on a seat | rebound damage | wins v / s / j | ties | timeouts |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| before | 0 | 0 | 0 | 78 / 51 / 51 | 0 | 0 |
| after | 4,642 | 2,579 | 7,985 | 72 / 57 / 51 | 0 | 0 |

Mean match length moves both ways, by at most 13% (8 seats, 2,032 to 2,293 ticks). The rebound column
counts every rebound that landed on a seat, a mob's turned bullet included. That is why its
damage is above one per hit, although a rival blaster's rebound deals 1.

### Tests

- `ai/parry.test.ts`, 21 cases. Each rule has a twin that must not act:
  - the bullet pick's share, and that it is independent per seat;
  - the look-ahead edge;
  - a bullet inside the reach against one moving away;
  - the skipped kinds (unpicked, rebound, own side, still);
  - a bullet from behind, outside the arc;
  - the press edge;
  - the frozen cooldown;
  - swapping back only once recovered;
  - swinging at a body in reach;
  - a bullet whose line passes wide of the reach;
  - a blade mid-swing, which still turns a bullet, against one only recovering;
  - the press edge on the swap back, and the swap back with no hostile left;
  - a dead or downed bot, which presses nothing;
  - a revive under fire. Its control is the same bullet with nobody to revive, which is
    parried.
- An engine duel shows the shipped bot really batting a rival's bullets back, with rebounds
  landing on the shooter. The control, `parries: false`, turns nothing.
- Mutations: dropping the INTERACT guard, the arc test, the press edge or the recovery wait each
  turn a test red, as does a share of 100. A second battery after the push found six more
  that survived (the wide-line skip, the mid-swing readiness, the swap-back press edge, the
  no-hostile body test, and the dead and downed guards); each now has a case and is killed.
  The same pass removed a dead `leave < 0` check: a closing bullet cannot have left the reach.
- `server/test/onlineBotParry.test.ts`: the bot through a clocked `MatchRoom` at the shipped
  batch size must parry at least 60% as often as offline, with both sessions' hashes equal. The
  control is a 3-frame cushion, which fails the gate. Setting `BOT_BUFFER_FRAMES` to 3 turns the
  gate red (11 against a bar of 18).
- `pvpBalanceSim.sim.ts` now reports parries and rebounds, and gates that both happen. With the
  rule off it fails.
- `BotClient.test.ts`: the reference human session now runs at 0 frames of cushion, as a browser
  tab does. Under the old default it trailed the bot by three frames, and the hash comparison
  failed on frame count.
