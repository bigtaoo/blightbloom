## Twelve voices is what real play needs, measured (2026-09-29, audio + tools + test + docs, no game code change)

`design/11` had listed the voice cap as open since 2026-08-27: `CueMixer.DEFAULT_CAP` is 12,
and its own comment called that "a first pass, not a measurement". The cap is a device budget,
and nothing here can measure a phone. What can be measured is the other half: how many sample
voices real play asks for, and which cues pay when it asks for more than the cap. This pass
built that measurement. **Twelve is the smallest cap at which no cue from `impact` up loses a
voice in any mode played.** So the number stays, and it is now gated.

### How it is measured

`client/sim/voiceDemand.sim.ts` (`npm run test:voice-sim`, folded into `test:sims`, about a
minute) plays headless matches and records what the **real** `EventReactor` plays for one seat.
The reactor's fx, HUD and actor views are no-op proxies, and its audio bus is the recorder.
Coalescing is therefore the game's own: a tick of ten hits is one `impact` here, exactly as in
the game. The log is then replayed through the **real** `VoiceBudget` at a ladder of caps.
Each voice lasts as long as the shipped variant it would draw, read off the mp3 with
`parseMp3` (`sim/audio/voiceDemand.ts`).

Two checks that the model is the game:

- **Durations.** In a real browser (the dev build, a hooked `CueMixer`), every live claim's
  length matched the decoded buffer, and the decoded buffer matched `parseMp3`'s audible
  length to the millisecond: `muzzle` 0.140, `death.enemy` 0.600, `spawn` 0.400. The only
  difference is the ±3% pitch jitter, which the replay ignores.
- **The live budget.** A 90 s unkillable browser run, firing throughout, made 503 claims, all
  of them samples (0 synth). It peaked at 6 voices, p99 5, and refused nothing. That run
  cleared its room in 9 s and then fired at nothing, so it validates the plumbing rather than
  measuring demand.

The `spawn` cue has no event behind it (`Scene.spawnedActors`). The recorder approximates it
as living players and enemies whose id it has not seen before.

### What real play asks for

Eight modes. The PvE ones are 40 seeds each of the shipped level 1 with the PvE bot. The PvP
ones are 10 bot-vs-bot matches each, with skins shuffled per seed and the listening seat
rotated. **Unkillable** pins hp every tick, so the run reaches the boss floor: all 40 unkillable
co-op runs reached the last floor and won.

| Mode | Cues | Uncapped peak | p99 | Lost at 8 | Lost at 10 | Lost at 12 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PvE careful | 29,851 | 12 | 7 | 53 (0.18%) | 5 (0.02%) | 0 |
| PvE aggressive | 5,147 | 10 | 7 | 15 (0.29%) | 0 | 0 |
| PvE co-op | 140,097 | 13 | 8 | 779 (0.57%) | 48 (0.03%) | 2 |
| PvE unkillable | 182,220 | 13 | 8 | 553 (0.31%) | 42 (0.02%) | 2 |
| PvE co-op, unkillable | 170,963 | 14 | 8 | 777 (0.46%) | 52 (0.03%) | 5 |
| PvP 2 seats | 4,621 | 12 | 10 | 52 (1.13%) | 19 (0.41%) | 0 |
| PvP 4 seats | 6,194 | 11 | 10 | 76 (1.23%) | 19 (0.31%) | 0 |
| PvP 8 seats | 3,530 | 7 | 5 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

"Lost" is refused plus stolen. At 12, every voice lost across all eight modes is a `muzzle`.

- **Demand is 8 voices at p99 and 14 at worst.** The cap binds only on the rare peak, and at
  12 the peak costs nine `muzzle`s in about 540,000 cues.
- **Below 12, `impact` starts to pay.** At 11, PvP 2 seats loses 2 `impact`s. At 10, four modes
  lose some (17 and 18 in the two small PvP modes, 1–2 in two PvE modes). At 8, every mode but 8-seat PvP does.
  `impact` is the first cue above the three that only texture a shot the player already sees
  (`muzzle`, `swing`, `clash`).
- **PvP asks for more than PvE at p99** (10 against 7–8). Cues are not spatial, so a seat hears
  every shot in the arena. With 8 seats the matches are short, and fights spread across squads.

### What this changes

- **`DEFAULT_CAP` stays at 12.** Its comment now cites this measurement instead of "a first
  pass". The number reasoned from what a frame can ask for turns out to be the measured minimum
  for these bots, with no headroom above it.
- **Two gates in `voiceDemand.sim.ts`**, over every mode, at the shipped cap:
  - the cap may cost real play at most 0.1% of its voices;
  - no cue at or above `impact`'s priority may lose one.

  A third case proves both gates can fail: a cap of 4 breaks both. What they guard is the next
  change that raises demand, such as a longer sample, a new cue or a denser room, rather than
  today's content.
- **`sim/audio/voiceDemand.test.ts`**, 10 cases in the default suite, covers the recorder and
  the replay. A mutation battery of 6 ran over them: the cut-length clock, variant cycling, the
  seen-id guard, the alive filter, the synth skip and the cap. All 6 are killed.
- `check.yml`'s comment on the `sims` job now names all five suites. It said three, and
  `test:weapon-sim` had already made it four.

### Still open

- **The device half.** Whether a low-end phone can mix 12 voices, and what `InnerAudioContext`
  or `WebAudioContext` costs there, still needs a real WeChat device. If it cannot, this pass
  says what a lower cap would cost, row by row.
- **Bots are not people.** A human who stands and fights may ask for more than a kiting bot.
  The co-op unkillable mode is the densest stand-in here. A recorded human run
  (the replay files `client/sim/replay/` reads) fed through `CueLog` would answer it.
